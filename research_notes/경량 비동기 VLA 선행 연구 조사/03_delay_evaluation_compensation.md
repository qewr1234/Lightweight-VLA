# Inference delay / observation staleness in action-chunking policies and VLAs: how it is evaluated and compensated (state of the literature as of 2026-11-16)

Verification legend used throughout: **[V]** = read in the paper's own full text (arXiv HTML) or in the repository source at a pinned commit; **[A]** = abstract page only; **[S]** = secondary summary (search snippet, listing page, unreviewed PR) not checked against a primary full text. Numbers marked "~" were read off figures by the cited paper's own prose, not from a table.

Notation: d = inference delay in control steps (d = floor(latency / control period)), H = chunk length, s/K/e = execution horizon. "Generation-side" = changes what the model generates (input conditioning, denoising loop, weights). "Execution-side" = post-processes or re-indexes an already generated chunk in the control loop with no model call.

## 1. What does arXiv 2605.08168 do exactly?

### Takeaway
It is a single-author bachelor-thesis benchmark (not a new method) that re-implements IT-RTC, TT-RTC, VLASH and A2C2 in two unified codebases and sweeps a **fixed integer delay** d (up to 15 on Kinetix, up to 20 on LIBERO with SmolVLA, H=50, s=50); delay is injected by feeding a d-step-old observation, never by measured or variable latency. Its own limitations paragraph names the fixed-d assumption and the lack of physical-robot evaluation as open, and only one of its four methods (IT-RTC) is training-free.

This latency is reported as a cost table and is **not** fed back into the closed-loop evaluation. — [arXiv 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

The paper notes "the prefill can in principle be pipelined with action execution" while IT-RTC's overhead "falls entirely on the (sequential, non-pipelinable) denoising loop". — [arXiv 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

- [V] Limitations paragraph, **verbatim**: "Our evaluation treats d as a fixed integer; in real deployments d varies with GPU scheduling, network jitter, and input complexity, conditions under which delay-aware methods (TT-RTC, A2C2) likely have an additional advantage we do not capture. Hyperparameters were taken from the original papers and not individually retuned, as our goal was to get a first understanding of how these recent asynchronous inference approaches compare, rather than developing a new method. Following the VLASH authors' own LIBERO setup, our VLASH results rely on oracle future states to bypass the action/state dimension mismatch that prevents the additive rollforward used in the original VLASH formulation (and possible on Kinetix); a deployable variant would require either a lightweight learned forward model or an environment patch restoring that coupling. - [V] Future-work statement on composition (Discussion), verbatim: A2C2's "design composes naturally with the other three methods, a direction we view as promising and did not exhaustively explore." — [arXiv 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

### Inferences
- The benchmark covers exactly one training-free fix (IT-RTC); it contains no execution-side training-free method, no composition of fixes, no variable delay, and no separation of vision-encoding time from chunk-generation time even though its own FLOPs table shows the two differ by ~9:1 for SmolVLA.
- With s = 50 = H, the LIBERO protocol is "execute the entire chunk"; results therefore mix observation staleness with long open-loop execution, and the d=0 baseline (~61%) is far below the SmolVLA numbers other papers report with short horizons (e.g. 79.0% sync in VLASH), so absolute numbers are not comparable across papers.
- The paper's VLASH caveat is direct evidence that in LIBERO a state-space roll-forward from delta actions is awkward; an action-space correction (idea B) sidesteps the state/action dimension mismatch.

### Gaps
- No full results table exists in the paper (LIBERO numbers are prose approximations of a figure); exact per-suite values would have to be read from Figure 13 or regenerated from the released code.
- The paper has no separate "future work" section beyond the sentences quoted above.

## 2. The Real-Time Chunking family and relatives: per-paper notes

### Takeaway
Almost every method acts on the **generation side** (guidance or blending inside the denoising loop, input conditioning, fine-tuning, adapters, candidate selection), and almost every simulation study injects delay as a **fixed integer number of control steps**; the gradient-free, training-free members (SimpleRTC, Leap-VLA soft-nudge, FreeBar, BID, PACT-VLA, FASTER) still all require running or modifying the model's sampling loop. Purely execution-side, model-free handling exists only as prefix dropping (UMI, DynamicVLA LAAS, lerobot), blending/splines (temporal ensembling, lerobot blending, VLA-RAIL), index re-matching (Mobile UMI) and learned residuals (A2C2).

**Per-paper notes**

- **RTC — "Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies"**, Black, Galliker, Levine, arXiv:2506.07339 (v2 5 Dec 2025; NeurIPS checklist present). [V] Method: asynchronous chunking as inpainting — freeze the first d actions of the new chunk to the previous chunk's committed actions and softly guide the overlap with pseudoinverse (VJP) guidance at every denoising step, with a clipped guidance weight β. The runtime "forecasts the next delay, d, by keeping a buffer of past delays" and uses max of the buffer (conservative estimate). Training-free; flow/diffusion policies only. Sim: 12 Kinetix tasks (force control, "no concept of holding position"), H=8, 4-layer MLP-Mixer, 2048 rollouts per point, "simulate delays between 0 (fully closed-loop) and 4". Real: π0.5, H=50, 50 Hz, 5 denoising steps, model latency 76 ms baseline vs 97 ms with RTC, LAN adds 10–20 ms, so d≈6; plus +100 ms and +200 ms injected latency (d≈11, d≈16); RTC shows no throughput degradation with injected delay, synchronous degrades linearly, temporal ensembling triggers protective stops at +100/+200 ms. Footnote: "we do not consider delays or synchronization issues at the sub-timestep level". Stated limitations: "it adds significant computational overhead compared to methods that sample directly from the base policy, and it is applicable only to diffusion- and flow-based policies." — [arXiv 2506.07339](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07339)
- **Training-time RTC — "Training-Time Action Conditioning for Efficient Real-Time Chunking"**, Black, Ren, Equi, Levine, arXiv:2512.05964 (v2 9 Dec 2025). [V] Method: simulate delay at training time, feed a clean ground-truth action prefix with per-token flow timesteps and compute loss on the postfix only; zero inference overhead, "a few additional lines of code". Needs fine-tuning (real-world: 8,000 steps, batch 512, π0.6 on box building and espresso making). Sim: same Kinetix setup, fixed d 0–4, 2048 trials per point; outperforms inference-time RTC at d ≥ 2, marginally worse at d = 0, 1. Limitations verbatim: "training-time RTC is fundamentally less flexible than inference-time RTC; it only supports conditioning on a 'hard' action prefix corresponding to the inference delay ... Additionally, training-time RTC requires carefully choosing the distribution of delays to simulate at training time based on the expected inference latency." — [arXiv 2512.05964](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05964)
- **SimpleRTC — HoloBrain-0 Technical Report**, arXiv:2602.12062 (12 Feb 2026). [V] SimpleRTC "is a gradient-free method that requires neither model modification nor retraining"; it uses "the unexecuted segment of the preceding action chunk to guide the denoising phase", enforcing "strict consistency with the preceding chunk for the initial d steps (corresponding to the inference latency), followed by a smooth trajectory fusion within a subsequent transition window of length L". Paired with teacher-forcing training (prefix length ~ Poisson(λ) matched to expected delay, ratio e.g. 25%) to close the clean-prefix train/test gap. The RoboOrchard client "feeds back execution latency and unexecuted action history to the model server". Evaluation: real-robot cloth folding ablation only (figure, no delay sweep); SimpleRTC async beats synchronous in success and completion time. Asynchronous inference frequency stated as "typically 5–10 FPS". — [arXiv 2602.12062](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12062)
- **REMAC — "Real-Time Robot Execution with Masked Action Chunking"**, Wang et al., arXiv:2601.20130 (27 Jan 2026; cited by PACT-VLA as ICLR 2026). [V] Method: LoRA (rank 4) on a pretrained policy, trained with a delay-conditioned prefix mask (d ~ uniform), a self-conditioned curriculum and an extra loss, plus prefix-preserved sampling (initialise from the executed prefix and keep it fixed); LoRA can be merged so there is no inference overhead. Identifies "intra-chunk inconsistency" (the d inherited actions are conditioned on the older observation). Sim: Kinetix, H=8, d 0–4, execution horizon averaged over max(1,d)…9−d; ablation Table 1 naive 0.828/0.702/0.639/0.525/0.451 vs REMAC 0.888/0.879/0.859/0.817/0.779 for d=0…4. Robustness test (Appendix F): true delay fixed at d but the policy is told a wrong delay sampled from {d−1,d,d+1}, plus 10% "spiky" maximum-delay values; RTC 0.817/0.778/0.733/0.665/0.588 vs REMAC 0.877/0.860/0.832/0.796/0.760 clean. Real: π0 at 15 Hz, h=8, model 76–80 ms + LAN 34–40 ms + processing 10–20 ms = 122–140 ms (d=2–3), plus +75/+150 ms injections; "RTC suffers from the additional inference delay it introduces". Limitations verbatim: "it requires specifying a maximum inference delay in advance ... If the actual delay during execution exceeds this bound, unexpected failure may occur. Second, the approach may demand a substantial amount of finetuning data". — [arXiv 2601.20130](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20130); venue per [PACT-VLA reference list](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28019)
- **Soft RTC — "Action-Prior Denoising for Smooth Real-Time Chunking"**, Liu et al., arXiv:2605.25537 (25 May 2026). [V] Method: generalises training-time RTC by replacing the binary prefix mask with continuous token-wise overlap weights; overlap tokens are corrupted from partially denoised (prior-informed) states in training and blended with the aligned previous chunk before each solver step at inference ("lightweight token-wise prior blending", no guidance). Needs one-epoch fine-tune. Sim: 12 Kinetix levels with released RTC assets, d 0–4, "high delay" = d ∈ {3,4}. Numbers: Train Soft RTC (N=1) overall solve 0.809 vs Train Hard RTC 0.815 (bootstrap intervals overlap); N=2 solve 0.781 with high-delay action delta 1.403→1.276 and jerk 2.416→2.185 (−9.1% / −9.6%). Real: small sorting study; OpenPI deployment latency 60.6 ms base vs ~87 ms for RTC-trained checkpoints. The paper states Hard RTC "remains the strongest single point if the only objective is maximizing average task success". — [arXiv 2605.25537](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25537)
- **FutureRTC — "Real-Time Robot Execution with Anticipatory-Conditioned Action Chunking"**, arXiv:2607.24008 (27 Jul 2026; Sichuan Univ., UESTC, Univ. of Alberta). [V] Method: plug-and-play modules around a frozen flow VLA — a state-correction module on top of the additive roll-forward s̃ = s ⊕ Σ a (compensating the gap between rolled-forward and actual state), an observation-prediction module that forecasts execution-time visual latents by motion-aware feature transport, and a policy-consistency loss; the chunk is then "executed directly from its first action". Modules are trained (RTX 4090, batch 128, 200k iterations). Sim: Kinetix d ∈ [0,4]; LIBERO d ∈ {5,10,15,20} with π0.5 and SmolVLA-450M. LIBERO π0.5 success at d=5/10/15/20: naive 89.5/82.7/76.3/68.3, TE 87.8/84.3/80.6/74.8, BID 90.3/84.0/76.1/71.3, RTC 89.9/83.2/77.5/73.7, T-RTC 89.4/84.0/77.9/74.0, VLASH 88.0/83.4/79.5/74.5, REMAC 90.8/85.4/78.5/73.9, FutureRTC 94.2/92.4/91.0/88.5. SmolVLA-450M: naive 70.3/64.5/61.3/56.2, RTC 69.7/64.9/62.6/58.7, FutureRTC 75.8/73.3/71.6/69.4. Limitations verbatim (excerpt): the observation predictor "may struggle in highly dynamic scenes dominated by independent external agents"; extending to "autoregressive or discrete-diffusion policies, and more extreme inference delays remains an interesting direction for future work". — [arXiv 2607.24008](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24008)
- **VLASH — "Real-Time VLAs via Future-State-Aware Asynchronous Inference"**, Tang et al. (MIT Han Lab), arXiv:2512.01031 (v2 26 Jul 2026). [V] Method: roll the proprioceptive state forward through the already-committed actions and condition the policy on (stale image, future state); fine-tune with temporal-offset augmentation so the model trusts state over image; optional action quantization for speed. Sim: Kinetix (12 tasks, 1,024 rollouts per point, "simulated delays ranging from 0 to 4 steps"; future state obtained by rolling the simulator forward with independent noise) — d=4: 81.7% vs 51.2% naive. LIBERO (30K fine-tune iterations, K=5): π0.5 sync 96.8 → VLASH 97.2/97.1/94.6 at d=1/2/3; SmolVLA sync 79.0 → 77.1/78.1/79.1. Authors note "Since LIBERO tasks involve slowly changing environments with mild state transitions, different asynchronous methods behave similarly", so they only compare to sync there. Real: Galaxea R1 Lite and SO-101, laptop RTX 5090, 30 Hz, K=24, "inference delay of 2 steps"; π0.5 latency 23.2/29.4/32.3 ms (H100/5090/4090) vs RTC 48.4/49.6/54.2 ms. — [arXiv 2512.01031](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01031)
- **A2C2 — "Leave No Observation Behind: Real-time Correction for VLA Action Chunks"**, Sendai et al., arXiv:2509.23224 (27 Sep 2025). [V] Method: a lightweight correction head that "runs every control step and adds a time-aware correction to any off-the-shelf VLA's action chunk", conditioned on the latest observation, base action, time-in-chunk feature and base-policy features. Execution-side but **learned** (trained on base-policy rollouts; base frozen). Sim: Kinetix (12 tasks, 2048 rollouts) with fixed d and horizon sweeps; LIBERO-Spatial with SmolVLA-450M. Numbers: Kinetix "+23% point" over RTC and ~+35 points over naive at d=4; LIBERO-Spatial naive 81.8% (e=10, d=0) vs A2C2 89.2%; naive 64.4% (e=40, d=10) vs A2C2 84.2%. — [arXiv 2509.23224](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23224)
- **PACT-VLA — "Speculative Action-Chunk Verification with Pre-Commit Observations"**, Zhu et al., arXiv:2610.28019 (20 Oct 2026). [V] Method: training-free; generate N candidate chunks from the inference-start observation, then score them with the frozen policy under a later "pre-commit" observation o_{t+d−1} (FlowRes = residual between each candidate's implied flow direction and the predicted velocity) and commit the best; pre-commit observation encoding is pipelined so selection stays in the same delay bucket. Sim delay: fixed d; Kinetix H=8, replans every max(1,d), 2,048 rollouts; LIBERO 500 episodes per suite and delay, executes first 5 (π0.5) or 8 (GR00T N1.7) actions. Kinetix d=1/2/3/4: naive 75.1/71.1/58.7/48.9; BID 87.9/81.2/68.7/55.8; RTC 89.3/84.8/75.0/62.2; TT-RTC 88.5/86.0/80.0/72.1; PACT 91.1/89.4/85.6/77.2. Composes with trained methods: VLASH 84.7→87.5 avg, DEFLECT 88.6→90.1, REMAC 83.7→85.2. LIBERO single-candidate async averages: π0.5 94.2/87.8/76.3/60.0 at d=1/2/3/4 (PACT 93.9/89.8/79.7/63.5); GR00T N1.7 93.4/84.8/69.6/51.2 (PACT 92.8/87.1/74.3/56.4). Real: π0.5 on bimanual AgileX PiperX, H=50, 30 Hz, 50 trials per task: naive 60.0%, VLASH 66.0%, Old-FlowRes 64.7%, PACT 82.0%. Measured P99 latency RTX 5090 35.4 ms (N=1) vs 60.2 ms (N=4 + verification), both d=2 at 30 Hz; Appendix C gives a staged timeline (prefill 23.2 ms, flow integration 24.9 ms, pre-commit prefill 25.2 ms overlapping, scoring 1.7 ms). — [arXiv 2610.28019](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28019)
- **FASTER — "Rethinking Real-Time Flow VLAs"**, Lu et al. (HKU, ACE Robotics), arXiv:2603.19199 (v3 16 May 2026). [V] Method: Horizon-Aware Schedule that denoises near-term actions first so the first action can be emitted after one step (reduces Time-To-First-Action), with a streaming client-server; analysis that reaction time is a uniformly distributed random variable set by TTFA and execution horizon. Real-world focus: π0.5 and X-VLA at 30 Hz on RTX 4090 and consumer RTX 4060 ("inference runs at merely 3Hz" on the 4060; 3× TTFA gain for X-VLA on 4060); table-tennis task. Limitations (Appendix G) verbatim excerpt: "our method is primarily applicable to flow-based or diffusion-based VLA models whose action generation process involves iterative sampling. The actual latency reduction also depends on the implementation and hardware." [S] A search summary describes it as needing "no architectural modifications or additional training"; I did not verify whether the real-robot checkpoints were fine-tuned with the schedule. — [arXiv 2603.19199](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19199)
- **TIDAL — "Temporally Interleaved Diffusion and Action Loop for Dynamic Manipulation"**, Sun et al. (A*STAR I2R), arXiv:2601.14945 (v3 14 Sep 2026). [V] Method: backbone-agnostic dual-frequency scheduler for GR00T-N1.5-3B — a low-rate macro loop caches VLM embeddings, a high-rate micro loop interleaves single-step flow integration with execution, trained with "temporally misaligned" samples ("We simulate variable inference latencies by creating training samples with randomized time-lags"). Delay model: "To quantify architectural latency, we use the latency numbers reported for NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin in [GR00T]"; cycle time 413 ms for the open-loop baseline (~2.4 Hz) vs ~9–10 Hz for TIDAL at 50 Hz control; evaluated under "Paused" vs "Non-Paused" physics ("physics advances during policy inference"). Numbers (Dynamic-Easy interception on a RoboCasa-based benchmark): open-loop baseline 0.31 paused → 0.09 non-paused; compute-heavy replan 0.63 → 0.17; TIDAL 0.61 → 0.30; static RoboCasa 0.6088 baseline vs 0.5938 TIDAL. Requires training. — [arXiv 2601.14945](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14945)
- **DEFLECT — "Temporal Counterfactual Preference Learning for Delay-Robust Asynchronous VLAs"**, Zhu et al. (HKUST-GZ, One Robotics), arXiv:2605.19294 (v3 20 Oct 2026). [V] Method: offline post-training that builds preference pairs from recorded trajectories (actions generated from stale vs execution-time inputs) and applies preference (DPO-style) supervision with an SFT loss. Sim: Kinetix d 0–4 (1,024 rollouts per task; 86.1% at d=4, +4.2 over VLASH); LIBERO d 1–4, 500 episodes per suite and delay. LIBERO GR00T N1.7: naive 92.25/84.90/69.25/50.40 at d=1/2/3/4 (avg 74.20) → DEFLECT 93.60/91.05/85.30/73.10 (avg 85.76); SmolVLA naive avg 49.77 → 60.26 (+14.0 at d=4); π0.5 (VLASH base) 95.54 → 96.28. Note: FreeBar's reference list cites the same arXiv work under a different title ("delay-robust execution via flow-matching likelihood-estimated counterfactual tuning"), so the title changed across versions. — [arXiv 2605.19294](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19294); alternate title in [FreeBar references](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14997)
- **Action ControlNet (ACNet)**, Guo & Guo, arXiv:2606.25985 (24 Jun 2026). [V] Method: delay-aware adapter that encodes the "executed motion suffix" (the d actions executed during inference) in a side branch and injects it residually into a mostly frozen action head; trains ~20% of parameters. Sim: Kinetix d 0–4 and Meta-World MT50 (H=50, e=25, d ∈ {0,5,10,15}, RTX 4080 SUPER), real SO-ARM101. - **Bidirectional Decoding (BID)**, Liu et al., arXiv:2408.17355 (v4 25 Apr 2025). [V] Test-time algorithm: sample multiple candidate chunks each step and select by backward coherence (agreement with previous decisions) and forward contrast (against a weaker checkpoint). Gradient-free, no retraining of the main policy, but needs batch sampling. The original paper studies closed-loop reactivity under stochastic noise, not inference delay; the RTC paper states "Liu et al. do not consider inference delay" and that BID with batch 16 on π0.5 "has 2.3 times the latency" of RTC. — [arXiv 2408.17355](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17355); [arXiv 2506.07339](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07339)
- **Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP)**, arXiv:2406.04806 (v4 11 Oct 2024). [V] Trained variant of diffusion policy that keeps a buffer whose immediate action is noise-free and later actions increasingly noisy, so each new observation needs only a few denoising steps on the rolled-over buffer. It is a speed-up of generation (requires its own training recipe); it does not model inference delay in evaluation. — [arXiv 2406.04806](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04806)
- **RNR-DP — "Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy"**, Chen et al. (UCSD), arXiv:2502.12724 (v2 13 Aug 2025; ICLR 2025). [V] Noise-relaying buffer with progressively increasing noise; one denoising step emits one clean action conditioned on the latest observation. Trained method. Reports +18% success over Diffusion Policy on response-sensitive tasks and +6.9% over the best acceleration method on regular tasks. No inference-delay model; stated limitation: "the lack of real-robot evalutions". — [arXiv 2502.12724](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12724)
- **Leap-VLA "soft-nudge"**, Yang et al., arXiv:2611.01011 (31 Oct 2026). [V] Soft-nudge is "an autograd-free" chunk-boundary correction applied **inside each Euler step of the flow sampler**: x_{t+δ} = x̂1 + η·w ⊙ (ℓ − x̂1), where x̂1 is the one-step endpoint prediction, ℓ the leftover prefix of the executing chunk and w the RTC three-segment prefix weights; the paper describes it as "the RTC guidance update with the Jacobian transpose replaced by the identity", "one fused multiply-add per step and action dimension: no backward pass, no extra forward pass, no added latency". It is training-free and gradient-free but generation-side (it needs the denoising loop and the leftover of the previous chunk; it does not use executed-vs-planned differences). Results: boundary/intra displacement ratio 1.907 (none) → 1.194 (RTC guidance) → 1.035 (soft-nudge) on a 200-trajectory LIBERO evaluation; with per-control-step re-planning (R=1, 0.05 s) on LIBERO-Spatial the asynchronous-trained policy goes 95.5 (default 0.5 s re-plan) → 91.4 (R=1) → 97.1 (R=1 + soft-nudge), the frequency-matched reference 61.4 → 26.9 → 39.2. Negative result (Appendix B): on a stale self-leftover no mechanism (hard inpainting, guidance, soft-nudge) forces execution onto a conflicting leftover; bridging never exceeds 2–3%. Limitations verbatim excerpt: "Inference-time boundary corrections ... smooth the deployment-typical self-leftover regime but cannot force execution to follow a leftover that genuinely conflicts with the fresh observation"; "our evaluation is confined to quasi-static simulation suites and a small real-robot set". The rest of Leap-VLA (a ~1 Hz VLM cache + 20–50 Hz action expert conditioned on the "measured age" of the cache) requires training; headline 95.2% at 1 s refresh and 94.5% at 2 s vs 96.3% frequency-matched across four LIBERO suites. — [arXiv 2611.01011](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- **FreeBar — "Barrier-Constrained Guidance For Real-time Asynchronous VLA Execution"**, arXiv:2610.14997 (10 Oct 2026). [V] Training-free plugin: a control-barrier-function-inspired consistency constraint between consecutive chunks, solved as a one-dimensional QP in closed form at each denoising step ("requires no backpropagation through the VLA action head"), progressively tightened over denoising and graded along the horizon. Generation-side. Sim: Kinetix (12 tasks, d ∈ [0,4]) naive 91.5% at d=0 → 49.0% at d=4; RTC 61.4% and FreeBar 68.3% at d=4; RoboCasa with GR00T and π0; overhead +7.7% vs RTC +98.0% on GR00T. Real: 30 Hz, RTX 4090, fixed d=2, conveyor-belt and whack-a-mole. Limitations verbatim: "FreeBar's performance depends on the reliability of the carry-over reference. Additionally, forced alignment on proximal part of action chunk reduces reactivity in highly dynamic scenarios." — [arXiv 2610.14997](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14997)

### Inferences
- "Gradient-free and training-free" is no longer a novelty claim by itself at the generation side: SimpleRTC (Feb 2026), FreeBar (Oct 2026) and Leap-VLA soft-nudge (Oct 2026) all replace RTC's VJP with a forward-only blend/projection inside the sampler; Soft RTC uses the same blending but with fine-tuning.
- All gradient-free generation-side methods target **chunk-boundary continuity** (agree with the leftover of the previous chunk). None of them corrects the remaining plan for the displacement accumulated while the chunk was being computed; Leap-VLA's Appendix B explicitly shows these mechanisms cannot move execution when leftover and fresh plan conflict.
- The executed-during-inference actions are widely used as a **model input** (VLASH roll-forward, FutureRTC state correction, ACNet "executed motion suffix", RAPAC-DP "pending actions", BICPO handoff roll, EagleVLA future latents) — always with training. Using the same quantity in closed form at the output is the unexplored corner (see section 3).
- Cross-paper numbers for "naive async" on Kinetix at d=4 are consistent (0.45–0.51 across REMAC, ACNet, PACT-VLA, FreeBar, VLASH), which makes Kinetix a usable common yardstick; LIBERO numbers are not comparable across papers because H, execution horizon and base checkpoints differ.

- HoloBrain-0 gives no numeric delay sweep for SimpleRTC (figure-only cloth-folding ablation); TT-RTC and RTC give Kinetix results only as figures, so per-delay numbers for those two come from re-evaluations in later papers.
- FASTER's simulation appendix (F.3/F.4) and whether its deployed checkpoints are fine-tuned with the Horizon-Aware Schedule were not read in detail.
- PAINT and DreamChunk full texts were not fetched; their descriptions are second-hand.
- "FutureRTC" was initially only found on an aggregator; the arXiv ID 2607.24008 was then confirmed from the arXiv HTML itself.

## 3. Is there prior work that corrects a stale chunk at execution time in closed form from the executed-vs-planned action difference? Relation to UMI, openpi and lerobot

### Takeaway
I found no paper or library that drops the first d actions of a stale delta-action chunk and then subtracts the accumulated (executed − planned) action difference from the following actions. The closest prior art splits into three partial overlaps: (i) prefix dropping with an absolutely anchored representation (UMI, openpi/lerobot state-relative chunks), which achieves the same re-anchoring implicitly but only when the action space is not per-step delta; (ii) Mobile UMI's training-free "state matching" for increment chunks, which re-indexes rather than offsets; and (iii) learned or generation-side uses of the same executed-action sum (VLASH, A2C2, ACNet, RAPAC-DP).

### Cited Findings
- [V] **UMI (arXiv 2402.10329)** handles latency at execution time by dropping: "The first few actions predicted are immediately outdated due to observation latency ..., policy inference latency ... and execution latency .... We simply discard the outdated actions and only execute actions with the desired timestamp after t_act for each hardware." It also measures per-stream latencies physically, aligns observations to the slowest stream, and sends commands ahead of time to compensate execution latency. — [arXiv 2402.10329](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329)
- [V] UMI's action representation: "Relative trajectory, used by UMI, is a sequence of end-effector (EE) poses relative to the same current EE pose for each inference step. In contrast, Delta action represents each action step relative to its immediate previous action, therefore accumulates error." Ablation on cup arrangement: relative trajectory vs delta action baseline 16/20 = 80% vs absolute 5/20 = 25% (calibration bias). Disabling latency matching on dynamic tossing drops success from 105/120 = 87.5% to 69/120 = 57.5%. — [arXiv 2402.10329](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329)
- [V] **openpi** `DeltaActions` subtracts the observation-time state from every action in the chunk (`actions[..., :dims] -= np.expand_dims(np.where(mask, state[..., :dims], 0), axis=-2)`) and `AbsoluteActions` adds it back at output; the config comment says "pi0 models are trained on delta actions (relative to the first state in each action chunk)". So openpi "delta" means state-relative chunk (UMI-style relative trajectory), converted back to absolute targets with the state that was sent with the request. — [openpi transforms.py @215abfb](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479/src/openpi/transforms.py); [openpi training/config.py @215abfb](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479/src/openpi/training/config.py)
- [V] The same openpi config states the exception that matters for LIBERO: "In Libero, the raw actions in the dataset are already delta actions, so we *do not* need to apply a separate delta conversion" and "LIBERO already represents actions as deltas". LIBERO chunks are therefore sequences of per-step controller deltas with no absolute anchor in the output transform. — [openpi training/config.py @215abfb](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/215abfb217dbac7d5f1273282331b9b1866c0479/src/openpi/training/config.py)
- [V] **lerobot (main, commit fe1c545, 13 Nov 2026) — RTC queue**: `ActionQueue._replace_actions_queue` "Discards the first `real_delay` actions since they correspond to the time spent during inference, when the robot was executing previous actions"; nothing else is done to the remaining actions. — [lerobot rtc/action_queue.py @fe1c545](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/fe1c54594f6ba275fccf210b9f7b11f0e2324b89/src/lerobot/policies/rtc/action_queue.py)

Before feeding it back to the policy, this helper re-expresses those actions relative to the robot's current joint state". This "re-anchoring" converts a leftover prefix into model input space for RTC; it is not an output correction and it applies to state-relative, not per-step delta, actions. — [lerobot rtc/relative.py @fe1c545](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/fe1c54594f6ba275fccf210b9f7b11f0e2324b89/src/lerobot/policies/rtc/relative.py); [lerobot relative_action_processor.py @fe1c545](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/fe1c54594f6ba275fccf210b9f7b11f0e2324b89/src/lerobot/processor/relative_action_processor.py)
- [V] **lerobot — remote/async inference (current)**: alignment is by sequence progress: "Let `c_obs` be the commitment cursor captured with an observation and `c_now` the cursor when its reply is accepted. The first `c_now - c_obs` predicted endpoints are no longer eligible. The runtime trims them and atomically replaces only the future queue." Optional client-side blending `blended = q_t + b_w * (i_t - q_t)` on the overlap, with "Relative-to-absolute processing occurs before blending". The README itself cautions: "Alignment establishes which temporal positions remain usable. It does not prove the robot physically matches the predicted state at those positions". - [V] **Mobile UMI (arXiv 2605.20894, 20 May 2026)** is the closest execution-side, training-free mechanism on increment actions. Its policy outputs per-step relative increments (Tp=16, Ta=8, 10 Hz). Measured latency 142 ms mean (std 18 ms: image 33, network inference 87, motor dispatch 22); i* mean 3.2 (std 1.1). The paper notes the matching "implicitly resets the integration baseline at every chunk". - [V] **DynamicVLA (arXiv 2601.22153)** "Latency-aware Action Streaming then discards latency-invalid action prefixes and executes only the temporally valid suffix of each predicted chunk"; newer chunks supersede older ones at overlapping steps. Prefix dropping only. — [arXiv 2601.22153](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22153)
- [V] **VLA-RAIL (arXiv 2512.24673, 31 Dec 2025)** is a training-free execution-side middleware: on arrival it computes "the latency-compensated index k*, discarding stale actions that correspond to past robot states", then blends the active trajectory into the new chunk with a dual-quintic spline for position/velocity/acceleration continuity, after cubic-polynomial intra-chunk smoothing. Operates on joint trajectories of an AgiBot G1; real-robot only (20 trials); stated limitation: fixed transition duration and need for tight clock synchronisation. No executed-vs-planned offset term. — [arXiv 2512.24673](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24673)
- [V] **PPC — "Training-Free Pace-and-Path Correction" (arXiv 2605.11459, v2 14 May 2026)** is a closed-form, training-free execution-side operator on delta end-effector chunks: corrected delta u_k = α*·Δp + δ_k*, where α* is a temporal compression factor and δ_k* a perpendicular offset profile that distributes "the perpendicular displacement gradually across the executed window rather than concentrating it on any single env-step", with boundary condition δ_K = 0. But its input is an **external object-velocity signal** (tracking/depth), its goal is moving-target compensation, and it is evaluated on its own MoveBench (ManiSkill, 20 Hz), not under inference delay; gains +16.4 to +28.8 points dynamic-only across π0, π0.5, SmolVLA, GR00T N1.6. — [arXiv 2605.11459](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11459)
- [V] **A2C2** is the execution-side counterpart that needs training: a learned residual added to every stale action using the latest observation. — [arXiv 2509.23224](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23224)
- [V] **VLASH** uses exactly the executed-action sum, but as model input: ŝ_{t+Δ} = s_t + Σ a_prev over the remaining actions of the previous chunk, for position-controlled robots; 2605.08168 notes "For velocity- or torque-controlled systems the rollforward is approximate". FutureRTC adds a learned "state correction module to compensate for the discrepancy between rolled-forward and actual execution-time proprioceptive states". — [arXiv 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168); [arXiv 2607.24008](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24008)
- [V] Generation-side, closed-form, gradient-free neighbours (SimpleRTC, Leap-VLA soft-nudge, FreeBar) all pull the new chunk toward the **leftover of the previous chunk** inside the sampler; none uses the difference between executed actions and the new chunk's own first d actions. — [arXiv 2602.12062](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12062); [arXiv 2611.01011](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); [arXiv 2610.14997](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14997)
- [S] An unreviewed PR in `zhang-jr/vla_server` (merged 10 Nov 2026) implements a `ChunkBuffer` with a default `trim` policy (drop stale queued actions) and a `linear_overlap` policy that "keeps executing stale actions"; it reports `linear_overlap` 15–19 points below `trim` at 150–500 ms on LIBERO-10 with π0.5. Again dropping/blending only. — [vla_server PR #1](https://github.com/zhang-jr/vla_server/pull/1)

### Inferences
- Idea B is mathematically the per-step-delta analogue of what UMI/openpi/lerobot get for free from a state-relative (observation-anchored) chunk: with absolute or state-relative targets, dropping the first d actions leaves the remaining targets on the intended absolute path regardless of what was executed meanwhile. With per-step delta chunks (LIBERO OSC deltas, as openpi's config confirms), dropping the first d actions shifts the whole remaining path by Σ(executed − planned) over those d steps, and no surveyed system removes that shift.
- Mobile UMI is the nearest published mechanism (forward-integrate the increment chunk from the observation-time state, compare with reality, discard). It differs from B in three ways: it needs a measured state and a hand-weighted state metric; it chooses a different **index** instead of keeping the latency-determined index and adding an **offset**; and it leaves any residual offset uncorrected. B needs only the action log, so it also works where state and action spaces do not match (the LIBERO problem documented in 2605.08168).
- PPC supplies precedent for "spread a closed-form correction over the next few delta actions with a decaying profile", but for a different disturbance source. A2C2 supplies precedent that execution-side correction of stale chunks is valuable (it is the strongest method at high delay on LIBERO in 2605.08168), but as a 20.8M-trainable-parameter head.
- Leap-VLA's negative result (boundary corrections cannot bridge a genuine conflict between leftover and fresh plan) is evidence that continuity-oriented fixes and drift compensation address different errors, which supports composing them.
- Risk for B's assumptions: action-sum equals displacement only for position-type delta control with good tracking; 2605.08168 and VLASH both flag roll-forward as approximate for velocity/torque control, and UMI flags that delta actions accumulate error.

### Gaps
- Not found: any paper, repository or documentation describing "drop d actions, then subtract Σ(executed − planned) spread over the next actions". Queries tried (WebSearch, standard and extended): "stale action chunk correction executed actions offset re-anchoring delta actions asynchronous inference VLA training-free"; "asynchronous VLA chunk handoff correction 'executed actions' minus 'planned actions' cumulative offset delta action chunk drift compensation no retraining"; "action chunk 'delta' actions asynchronous inference skip first actions accumulated displacement error compensate 'already executed' chunk switch offset correction robot policy latency"; plus source search in lerobot (`re-?anchor|stale|real_delay|inference_delay`) and openpi (`rtc|prefix|inference_delay`, no RTC code present at the pinned commit).
- Absence of evidence is not proof: classical tele-operation / trajectory-tracking literature and non-English or unpublished codebases were not searched, and the idea is simple enough that it may exist as an unnamed implementation detail in some deployment stack.
- I did not verify whether any LIBERO evaluation harness silently converts delta chunks to absolute targets before execution.

## 4. Which simulators/benchmarks model delay realistically? Has anyone replayed measured device latency or modelled a two-thread vision/action pipeline, or reported closed-loop success vs measured Jetson/edge latency?

### Takeaway
The standard practice is a fixed integer delay injected into Kinetix or LIBERO; realistic timing appears only in pieces: TIDAL's "non-paused" physics driven by published Jetson AGX Orin latency figures, EagleVLA's Jetson-Orin latency breakdown combined with a LIBERO delay sweep that separately accounts for action-expert time, a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper with a discrete-event executor using per-request measured latency, and dual-rate architectures that simulate a slow semantic loop. I found no work that replays measured edge-device latency traces (separate vision-encoding and chunk-generation times, variable per call) through a two-thread timeline inside LIBERO.

### Inferences
- Three levels of realism exist in the literature: (1) fixed integer d (nearly everything); (2) a constant latency taken from a device spec or measurement and converted to a schedule (TIDAL, EagleVLA, PACT-VLA's d=2, TBD-VLA's "0.119 s → 2 steps"); (3) per-request measured latency inside an event-driven executor (the RoboPAD workshop paper; presumably on a workstation GPU with an added constant). None reaches (4): replaying recorded edge-device traces with separate vision and generation stage times and their jitter.
- EagleVLA is the closest to idea A on the "edge device + LIBERO" axis: it has real Jetson Orin stage timings and a LIBERO delay sweep, and even splits VLM time from action-expert time — but through a fixed 3:1 ratio and integer Δ, with a trained correction module rather than training-free fixes, and for π0.5 rather than a lightweight VLA.
- The Jetson numbers in EagleVLA (1.4 s for π0.5 on Orin-50W; 2.3 s at 30 W) imply delays of tens of control steps at 20–50 Hz — far beyond the d ≤ 4 regime most methods were tuned for and comparable to the d = 15–20 regime where 2605.08168 shows IT-RTC collapsing to naive on SmolVLA.
- 2605.08168's own data (vision prefill ≈ 90% of SmolVLA compute, "can in principle be pipelined") is an explicit pointer toward a two-thread vision/action timeline that nobody has evaluated in closed loop.

### Gaps
- The RoboPAD workshop paper's full text and code (anonymous.4open.science link returned HTTP 403) were not readable; it is unknown whether its measured latency is replayed, sampled live, per-stage, or from an edge device. Treat its "measured latency" claim as abstract-level only.
- Not found: any paper reporting LIBERO (or other simulator) success as a function of measured Jetson Orin Nano / Orin NX latency for a lightweight VLA such as SmolVLA; queries tried: "Jetson Orin VLA measured inference latency closed-loop success rate LIBERO asynchronous delay injected edge device"; "VLA evaluation simulator real-time latency-aware closed-loop 'physics advances during inference' LIBERO wall-clock inference time benchmark"; "variable stochastic inference delay action chunking policy evaluation latency jitter distribution robot simulator real-time benchmark 2026".
- [S] A Chef Robotics blog post (May 2026) was reported by search to sample delay "from an empirically measured delay profile (normal distribution)" for training augmentation; not fetched or verified.
- vla.cpp (arXiv 2606.08094) and the `hous-lab/pi05-orin-edge` repository (claimed 92 ms p50 and 92% LIBERO-10 on AGX Orin) were surfaced by search but not read; both appear to be synchronous evaluations.

## 5. How large are the reported success-rate losses from delay, and which fixes recover how much?

### Takeaway
On dynamic Kinetix, naive async consistently loses roughly 40 points between d=0 and d=4 (≈0.83–0.92 → ≈0.45–0.51), training-free generation-side fixes recover about a third to half of that (RTC ≈ 0.61–0.62, FreeBar 0.68, PACT-VLA 0.77) and trained methods most of it (0.70–0.86). On quasi-static LIBERO the loss depends heavily on base model and execution horizon: from ~1–3 points for well-tuned π0.5 pipelines at d ≤ 2 up to 35–50 points at d=4 for GR00T N1.7/π0.5 with short horizons, or at d=20 for SmolVLA with full-chunk execution.

### Inferences
- Reported results conflict on which fix works at long delays on LIBERO. 2605.08168 finds IT-RTC collapsing to naive beyond d=8 and (oracle) VLASH flat on SmolVLA; EagleVLA finds the opposite ordering on π0.5 (RTC 88.8% vs VLASH 30.1% at Δ=9); FutureRTC finds RTC, T-RTC, VLASH and REMAC all within ~2–6 points of naive on π0.5 for d=5–20. The differences track chunk length/execution horizon (H=50, s=50 vs H=20 vs short horizons), oracle vs rolled-forward state, and base checkpoint, so a new evaluation must fix these explicitly and should not expect published rankings to transfer.
- For SmolVLA specifically, three independent papers show large delay sensitivity and weak recovery from training-free RTC: 2605.08168 (~61 → ~10–12 at d=20; RTC ~20), FutureRTC (70.3 → 56.2 at d=20; RTC 58.7), DEFLECT (naive average 49.77 over d=1–4). This is the regime where an additional training-free fix has measurable headroom.
- The training-free ceiling so far on Kinetix d=4 is PACT-VLA's 77.2% (4 RTC-guided candidates), still ~9 points below the best trained method (DEFLECT 86.1%) and ~14 below d=0.
- Small d (1–2) costs little on LIBERO with strong base models (≤ 2–6 points); an evaluation aimed at edge devices needs the large-d regime (EagleVLA's Orin timings imply ≥ 9–40 steps at 20–30 Hz for π0.5-class models) to show an effect.

### Gaps
- No paper reports the delay loss for a delta-action policy separately from an absolute/state-relative one on the same benchmark, so the fraction of the LIBERO loss attributable to delta-chunk drift (idea B's target) versus stale perception is unknown.
- Exact RTC and TT-RTC Kinetix numbers from the original papers exist only as figures; values in the table are re-evaluations by later authors.
- HoloBrain-0 SimpleRTC, FASTER and πR² report no simulated success-vs-delay curves.

## 6. Verdicts for idea A and idea B

### Takeaway
**Idea A — partially done.** Fixed-delay closed-loop benchmarking of async fixes on LIBERO with SmolVLA exists (2605.08168), and edge-device latency has been tied to LIBERO success through a fixed-ratio delay model (EagleVLA) or to non-paused physics through published device numbers (TIDAL); replaying measured, variable, per-stage edge latency through a two-thread timeline and composing training-free fixes under it is open. **Idea B — open as stated (no match found), with close neighbours.** Prefix dropping is standard and absolute re-anchoring is implicit in state-relative action spaces, but the explicit closed-form (executed − planned) offset correction for per-step delta chunks was not found in any paper or library searched.

### Cited Findings
- **Idea A, closest work and what each lacks**
  - 2605.08168: same benchmark family (LIBERO + SmolVLA, async methods compared), but "treats d as a fixed integer", measures latency only on an RTX 3090 as a side table, covers one training-free method, and leaves composition "not exhaustively explore[d]". — [arXiv 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)
  - EagleVLA: measured Jetson Orin/Thor stage latencies (ViT, LLM, action expert) and a LIBERO Δ = 1–9 sweep with Δ_ae = ceil(Δ/3), but integer fixed delays, π0.5, and a trained correction module. — [arXiv 2607.12659](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12659)
  - TIDAL: non-paused physics with Jetson AGX Orin latency figures, but a constant cycle time from a vendor report, a custom RoboCasa interception task, and a trained architecture. — [arXiv 2601.14945](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14945)
  - RoboPAD workshop paper: discrete-event executor with per-request measured latency + fixed +200 ms on LIBERO π0.5 with naive and RTC; edge device, stage split and variable-trace replay not indicated in the abstract. — [NeurIPS 2026 virtual page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/162917)
  - Leap-VLA / Acting While Understanding / πR²: two-rate vision/action pipelines, but the model is trained for staleness and the slow loop is a chosen period. — [arXiv 2611.01011](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); [arXiv 2606.15285](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15285); [arXiv 2607.26055](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26055)
  - Composition precedents: PACT-VLA stacks selection on RTC/VLASH/DEFLECT/REMAC (+1.5 to +2.8 points on Kinetix); REMAC reports modest gains when combined with BID and RTC; Leap-VLA combines staleness-aware training with soft-nudge. — [arXiv 2610.28019](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28019); [arXiv 2601.20130](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20130); [arXiv 2611.01011](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- **Idea B, closest work and what each lacks**
  - UMI: discards outdated actions; drift-free only because its chunks are relative to the observation-time pose (absolute after conversion). — [arXiv 2402.10329](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10329)
  - openpi / lerobot: state-relative chunks converted with the request-time state; lerobot RTC queue drops `real_delay` actions and nothing more; lerobot "re-anchor" refers to re-expressing an absolute leftover prefix for model input.   - VLA-RAIL: training-free execution-side drop + quintic blending on joint trajectories; continuity only. — [arXiv 2512.24673](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24673)
  - PPC: closed-form, training-free offsets spread across a delta chunk, but driven by external object velocity, not by executed-vs-planned actions or inference delay. — [arXiv 2605.11459](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11459)
  - A2C2: execution-side correction of stale chunks, but a trained head with fresh observations. — [arXiv 2509.23224](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23224)
  - VLASH / FutureRTC / ACNet / RAPAC-DP / BICPO: use the actions executed during inference, but as trained model conditioning. — [arXiv 2512.01031](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01031); [arXiv 2607.24008](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24008); [arXiv 2606.25985](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25985); [arXiv 2608.15924](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15924); [arXiv 2608.13924](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13924)

### Inferences
- **Exact remaining gap for A**: (1) record per-call latency traces on a real edge device, separately for vision encoding and chunk generation; (2) replay them (variable, not a constant d) in a discrete-event, two-thread executor inside LIBERO; (3) report success as a function of the device timeline for a lightweight VLA; (4) evaluate training-free fixes singly and composed under that timeline. Items (1)+(2) with stage separation and jitter, and (4) for execution-side fixes, have no precedent found; (3) has only the π0.5 fixed-ratio version in EagleVLA.
- Novelty risk for A is mainly positioning: reviewers can point to 2605.08168 (which lists the fixed-d limitation itself), EagleVLA and the RoboPAD workshop paper. The defensible claim is the measured, variable, stage-resolved timeline and what it changes relative to fixed-d rankings, not "first LIBERO delay benchmark".
- **Exact remaining gap for B**: an execution-side, model-free, closed-form rule for per-step delta chunks that keeps the latency-determined index and re-anchors the remaining plan to its intended absolute path using only the logged executed actions, with the correction spread over several steps. The contribution should be framed as "making delta-action chunks behave like observation-anchored relative trajectories under delay", citing UMI/openpi for the representation-level solution and Mobile UMI for the re-indexing alternative.
