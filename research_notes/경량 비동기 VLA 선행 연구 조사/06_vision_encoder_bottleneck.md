# Reducing the cost of the vision encoder itself in small VLAs and streaming ViTs (state of the literature as of 2026-11-16)

Provenance tags used on every finding:
- **[P]** = the primary page (arXiv HTML/abs/PDF, project page, repo, model card, conference page) was fetched in this session. Numbers were relayed through the fetch tool's summarizer, so spot-check before quoting in a paper.
- **[P-text]** = I read the extracted PDF text / table directly.
- **[S]** = known only from a search-result summary (secondary). Treat titles/IDs as reliable, numbers as unverified.

Notation: "ViT forward shortened?" = does the method make the vision-encoder forward pass itself cheaper (fewer patches, fewer layers, skipped attention), as opposed to only reducing tokens the LLM/action expert sees.

Reference setting for all "is it the same thing?" judgements: SmolVLA-class model (SigLIP at 512x512, 1024 patches/camera, pixel-shuffle to 64 tokens/camera, 2 cameras, ~450M params) on Jetson Orin Nano, where the vision tower (~260 ms for two cameras, project's own measurement) remains after the action expert is compiled, and where lowering resolution without retraining costs -28 points (384 px) / -50 points (320 px) on LIBERO-Spatial (project's own measurement).

## 1. Temporal reuse INSIDE a ViT across consecutive frames: has it been applied to robot policies / VLAs? Do VLA-Cache, TTF, EfficientVLA, FlashVLA, SP-VLA, SpecPrune-VLA skip any vision-encoder computation?

### Takeaway
Almost all "temporal redundancy" work in VLAs (VLA-Cache, LAC, TTF-VLA, SpecPrune-VLA, VLA-Pruner, EfficientVLA, FlashVLA, SP-VLA) acts on LLM-side tokens/KV states or on whole-step scheduling; the vision encoder still runs in full every step. The only VLA paper found that reuses computation *inside* the ViT is "Reducing Temporal Redundancy for Efficient VLA Inference" (arXiv 2607.12287, Jul 2026: pi0/pi0.5 at 224 px on A100, attention-only skip, ViT 40.1 -> 28.5 ms); nothing equivalent was found for a small VLA, for 512-px/1024-patch encoders, for per-camera policies, or on Jetson, and Eventful Transformers itself is not cited by the VLA papers checked.

### Cited Findings

**A. Video-ViT methods that do shorten the ViT forward (none evaluated on robot policies)**
- **Eventful Transformers** (arXiv 2308.13494, 25 Aug 2023, ICCV 2023; Dutson, Li, Gupta). Re-processes only tokens that changed significantly between frames; convertible from existing Transformers "often without any re-training"; compute budget adjustable at run time; "on the order of 2-4x" computational savings with minor accuracy loss on ImageNet-VID detection and EPIC-Kitchens-100 action recognition. The abstract page does not say whether 2-4x is FLOPs or wall-clock. ViT forward shortened: yes. Training-free: often. Robotics use: none found. [P] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13494)
- **DeltaCNN** (arXiv 2203.03996, CVPR 2022; Parger et al.). Sparse frame-difference inference for all typical CNN layers, no retraining, up to 7x over dense cuDNN on pose estimation / detection, with gains "more pronounced on lower-end hardware and smaller batch sizes"; motivated by fixed cameras. CNN-only, not ViT; no robotics application found. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03996)
- **Déjà Vu / ReuseViT** (arXiv 2506.14107, 17 Jun 2025, PVLDB 18; KAIST). Reuses QKV projections and FFN outputs for similar tokens across frames (self-attention itself is *not* reused); a learned 2-layer-MLP decision module plus a restoration MLP are trained (frozen ViT, "about an hour" to converge, Gumbel-Softmax). CLIP ViT embeddings; up to 1.81x / 2.64x / 2.54x throughput within a 2% error bound on retrieval / QA / grounding vs 1.32x / 2.08x / 2.20x for Eventful Transformer; RTX 3090. Reports that mixed reused/recomputed tokens create sparse GPU workloads requiring stream compaction, and cached activations raise memory. Notes that CMC and Eventful Transformer "lack GPU-optimized implementations", so their throughput was *interpolated*, not measured. ViT forward shortened: yes. Training-free: no (small modules). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14107v1)
- **STC-Cacher** (part of "Accelerating Streaming Video LLMs via Hierarchical Token Compression", arXiv 2512.00891, CVPR 2026; SJTU EPIC Lab). Full ViT pass on a reference frame caching per-layer K, V, attention outputs and MLP outputs; on other frames the lowest-cosine-similarity tokens (vs cached Keys) are recomputed and scattered into cached tensors. Default cache interval 4, reuse ratio 75%. Plug-and-play, no retraining. ViT encoding latency -24.5% to -34.7% (e.g. StreamForest 103.7 s -> 67.7 s for the benchmark run); accuracy cost e.g. OVO-Bench 54.3 -> 52.3 (StreamForest). Static caching (interval = infinity) collapses accuracy 43.7 -> 33.5 (feature drift). Hardware unspecified; robotics not mentioned. ViT forward shortened: yes. Training-free: yes. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00891)
- **ResidualViT** (arXiv 2509.13255, 16 Sep 2025; Soldan et al.). Interleaves full CLIP-ViT "I-features" with N cheap "P-features" computed from heavily token-dropped frames plus a learned residual token (single linear layer) distilled on WebVid-2.5M; N=2 cuts cost 56%, up to 2.5x faster; small accuracy drops (Charades-STA R@1@0.5 42.9 -> 41.5 with ViT-L/14). ViT forward shortened: yes. Training-free: no (tokenizer distilled). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13255)
- **CodecFlow** (arXiv 2604.06036) prunes redundant patches *before* the ViT using codec motion vectors, with Déjà Vu as a baseline. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06036v2)

**B. The one VLA paper that reuses computation inside the ViT**
- **"Reducing Temporal Redundancy for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Inference"** (arXiv 2607.12287v1, 14 Jul 2026; Wu, Zheng, ..., Linfeng Zhang, Chuan Wen). Odd/even scheme: odd chunks do a full ViT pass and cache key projections; even chunks compute cosine similarity between current and cached keys in the *first* ViT layer, select the top-rho-N least-similar tokens, and reuse those indices in subsequent layers. "Attention is computed only for updated tokens, while reused tokens directly inherit cached outputs. After residual addition, all tokens are processed by the MLP layer, ensuring full forward propagation and stable training." So only attention is skipped; the MLP runs on all tokens. [P-text] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12287)
  - Models/hardware: pi0 and pi0.5, 224x224 inputs, NVIDIA A100 (sim) and RTX 4090 (real), FP16, PyTorch. No Jetson/edge hardware. [P-text]
  - Table III (pi0.5, LIBERO): baseline ViT 40.1 ms / LLM 42.5 / action 212.6 / total 293.2 ms, SR 94.4; Token Reuse only: ViT 28.5 ms, total 282.9 ms, SR 93.9; with their 2-step policy: ViT 28.4, total 111.6 ms, SR 93.8. I.e. about -29% ViT time, but only -3.5% end-to-end when the action expert is not also compressed. [P-text]
  - Table VII update-ratio sweep (fraction recomputed -> mean SR): 1.0: 94.4; 0.2: 94.1; 0.3: 94.0; 0.4: 93.8; 0.5: 92.6; 0.6: 91.8. Success goes *down* as more tokens are recomputed, which is the opposite of what the accompanying text claims; treat this table with suspicion. [P-text]
  - Whether token reuse is applied training-free is not stated; Sec. II-C says feature consistency is maintained "through targeted training" and Sec. III-B mentions "stable training". Multi-camera handling (head + two wrist cameras on the real robot) is not described. Cites VLA-Cache; does not cite Eventful Transformers. Limitations stated only for the compressed policy (short-horizon validation). [P-text]
  - The paper's own conclusion is that the action expert dominates and that perception-side optimization gives "limited end to end gains when action generation dominates". [P-text]
- **Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA** (arXiv 2606.14409) adds a temporal pass inside the ViT every few layers, reusing existing QKV projections and discarding past-frame patch tokens in upper layers. This is a multi-frame-context design, not a compute-saving cache; details unverified (PDF too large to fetch). [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14409)

**C. VLA methods that are LLM-side or whole-step, not vision-encoder-side**
- **VLA-Cache** (arXiv 2502.02175, NeurIPS 2025; Xu et al.). Static patches are found by cosine similarity on *raw pixel patches* between consecutive frames (threshold + top-k), minus patches with high text-to-vision attention; their **KV states in the LLM decoder** are reused, with a layer-adaptive ratio. The paper says it "accelerates the VLA's language decoding process across timesteps"; it never states that the vision encoder is skipped. Training-free. OpenVLA LIBERO: SR 75.0 -> 74.7, FLOPs 1.864 -> 1.355 T, latency 51.91 -> 31.83 ms; OpenVLA-OFT 79.05 -> 62.59 ms (SR 96.8 -> 97.4); CogACT on SIMPLER 54.29 -> 39.63 ms; real Kinova Jaco2. RTX 4090, BF16. No vision-vs-LLM latency breakdown. Gains shrink in dynamic scenes; tested only on LLaMA2-based decoders; SR falls to 68.3% when 200 tokens are reused. ViT forward shortened: no (per the text). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)
  - SpecPrune-VLA's authors say VLA-Cache "only reduces 17–25% of total FLOPs" and measure it at 1.07x on OpenVLA-OFT in their own table. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05614v2)
- **LAC, "Learning to Accelerate VLA Models through Adaptive Visual Token Caching"** (arXiv 2602.00686). A small CNN token selector and a cache-ratio predictor (inputs: frame + RAFT-small optical flow) are trained with the VLA frozen; caching is at the **KV level inside the Transformer**. OpenVLA LIBERO 75.0 -> 76.9 SR, CUDA time 51.91 -> 29.51 ms (1.76x), FLOPs -25.3%; CogACT SIMPLER 1.42x. Stated limitation: fast camera ego-motion or global visual change forces recomputation of most tokens. ViT forward shortened: not stated (KV-level). Training-free: no. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00686)
- **TTF-VLA** (arXiv 2508.19257, AAAI 2026). Fuses previous-frame and current-frame patch tokens *after* the vision encoder (pixel-difference + attention relevance, keyframe every K=3 steps). Algorithm 1 calls the vision encoder on every frame, so nothing is skipped; the method adds "<2% runtime overhead" and its benefit is success rate (LIBERO OpenVLA 68.4 -> 72.4; SimplerEnv 33.2 -> 34.9; real Franka 38.3 -> 41.7). It names K/Q/V reuse for acceleration only as a future "free lunch" direction. ViT forward shortened: no. Training-free: yes. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19257v3)
- **SpecPrune-VLA** (arXiv 2509.05614; ICML 2026 poster listing). Prunes "60% to 70% visual tokens at the beginning of LLM forward" plus layer-level pruning in the LLM; patch similarity is computed on patches "before the image being encoded" but only to choose tokens. Training-free. OpenVLA-OFT and pi0; A800: 1.46x (OFT), 1.31x (pi0); RTX 3090: 2.09x on LLM time, 1.57x end-to-end; SR 96.6 -> 96.1 (OFT). States the LLM is >70% of end-to-end latency in the models profiled. ViT forward shortened: no. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05614v2)
- **VLA-Pruner** (arXiv 2511.16449; v5 dated 26 May 2026). Prunes at LLM layer K=3 using semantic and temporally smoothed action attention. Training-free. OpenVLA / OpenVLA-OFT / pi0; RTX 4090; 1.46x / 1.80x / 1.99x on OFT at 50 / 25 / 12.5% retention with ~101% / 98.4% / 88.3% relative success. ViT forward shortened: no. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16449v5)
- **EfficientVLA** (arXiv 2506.10100, NeurIPS 2025). Training-free: LLM layer pruning + task-aware visual token selection (from VLM attention) + diffusion-head feature caching; CogACT on SIMPLER, 1.93x speedup, FLOPs to 28.9%, -0.6% SR. Token selection uses VLM cross-attention, i.e. after the encoder. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10100v1)
- **SP-VLA** (arXiv 2506.12723, 15 Jun 2025). Model scheduling between the VLA and a lightweight action generator plus spatio-semantic token pruning; claims 1.5x (LIBERO) and 2.4x (SimplerEnv) lossless acceleration. The abstract does not say the encoder processes fewer inputs. [P, abstract only] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12723)
- **FlashVLA** (arXiv 2505.21200, 27 May 2025). Training-free action reuse + SVD-based visual token selection; LIBERO FLOPs -55.7%, latency -36.0%, SR -0.7%. The abstract does not locate the pruning relative to the encoder. [P, abstract only] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21200)
- **ElegantVLA** (arXiv 2605.29438, 28 May 2026; Tsinghua). A PPO-trained scheduler (base VLA frozen) picks a per-step level: level 1 refreshes the vision encoder but recomputes only first/last LLM layers; levels 2–4 **skip the vision encoder and LLM entirely for 1–3 steps**, reusing the cached vision-language representation. GR00T and CogACT; RTX 4090; real Franka control frequency 13.8 -> 26.3 Hz, latency 72.44 -> 38.00 ms. Caveat found in the text: a CogACT baseline of 89.60% vs ElegantVLA 72.54% in one table contradicts "preserved success". ViT forward shortened: no — it is skipped wholesale on some steps (staleness, not sparsity). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29438v1)

**D. Direct evidence on whole-prefix caching for a 450M VLA**
- **"Where Edge VLA Latency Actually Goes: Measured Scaling Laws and Staleness Admissibility for a 450M Vision–Language–Action Model"** (NeurIPS 2026 listing; Misra, Haque; no arXiv ID found). On a 450M flow-matching VLA (T4 and AGX Orin cited): caching the vision prefix across frames produced *more action error than INT8 quantization in all 24 moving conditions tested* (two synthetic stimuli); on a 1/f field both the stale-vision and stale-proprioception terms compound with reuse depth. The authors report action error as a fraction of action std, noting INT4 scores 0.977 cosine similarity but 6.9x the action error of INT8. Open-loop synthetic stimuli, not closed-loop task success. [P, conference abstract page] — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170035)

### Inferences
- "Token caching in VLAs" as published means LLM KV caching; for a SmolVLA-class model whose LLM prefix is only 64 tokens/camera and whose vision tower dominates, these methods attack the wrong component. This is consistent with SpecPrune-VLA's and 2607.12287's own profiles, which are of 3–7B models where the LLM or action expert dominates.
- 2607.12287 shows the Eventful/STC-Cacher idea transfers to a VLA ViT in principle (-29% ViT time at ~40% update ratio with ~0.5-point success change), but it (i) skips only attention, (ii) uses 256-token 224-px inputs where attention is a small share, (iii) reports only A100/4090 PyTorch numbers, and (iv) leaves training status ambiguous. At 1024 patches/camera, attention is a larger share of ViT cost, so the relative saving could be larger — untested.
- Whole-prefix caching (ElegantVLA levels 2–4; the dual-rate "cache the vision prefix" baseline) is the coarse limit of temporal reuse; the NeurIPS 2026 scaling-law paper suggests it is worse than INT8 under motion, which argues for *selective* (token- or camera-level) rather than all-or-nothing reuse.
- Wrist cameras break the "static patch" assumption: LAC and ActPrune both state that frame differencing degrades under camera ego-motion.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies Eventful-Transformer-style or STC-Cacher-style sparse token updates to SmolVLA / SigLIP-512 / any sub-1B VLA, or measures such reuse on Jetson. Queries tried: "Eventful Transformers temporal redundancy vision transformer applied robot policy VLA vision encoder caching"; "vision encoder temporal feature reuse across frames VLA skip ViT computation static patches caching robot policy"; "robot policy vision encoder Eventful OR delta OR incremental OR feature caching ViT consecutive frames reuse visuomotor policy speedup"; "SmolVLA vision encoder SigLIP caching sparse update static patches Jetson Orin Nano speedup".
- Whether 2607.12287's token reuse works without any fine-tuning is unknown (not stated in the paper text).
- Wall-clock (not FLOP) gains of sparse ViT updates on TensorRT/Jetson: no measurement found anywhere; Déjà Vu notes prior work lacked GPU-optimized implementations.
- "Skip-attention for static patches" as a named method: the only hit was Skip-Attention (arXiv 2301.02240), a single-image method; not investigated further.
- The NeurIPS 2026 "Where Edge VLA Latency Actually Goes" paper could not be read in full (OpenReview blocked); only the abstract-level page was available.

## 2. Token pruning/merging before or inside the vision encoder for VLAs / robotics VLMs (ToMe, LightVLA, VLA-Pruner, FastV-at-the-ViT, foveated / gaze / ROI ViTs); which need retraining?

### Takeaway
Nearly every VLA token-reduction paper prunes *after* the vision encoder (before or inside the LLM); the only VLA method found that drops patches *before* the encoder is ActPrune (arXiv 2611.08827, Nov 2026), which is training-free but needs calibrated cameras plus wrist depth, was tested on OpenVLA-OFT/pi0.5 only, and reports no encoder-only timing. Foveated designs that truly cut ViT compute (Look, Focus, Act: -94% tokens) require training the ViT from scratch and human gaze data and are not VLAs; VLA-side foveation work (FoveaVLA, ROI-driven attention) adds crops and tokens rather than saving compute.

### Cited Findings

**A. Pre-encoder (ViT forward shortened)**
- **ActPrune** (arXiv 2611.08827v2, 11 Nov 2026; Ewha Womans Univ.). Uses the previous step's predicted action chunk as a geometric prior *before* encoding. Third-person view: projects the anticipated end-effector trajectory with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and keeps kappa x kappa patch neighbourhoods (larger when the gripper is closed) plus per-episode "semantic anchors" from first-frame decoder attention. Wrist view: reprojects previous-frame patches via wrist RGB-D depth and action-implied camera motion, prunes the most unchanged patches, and averages pruned patches into a 2x2 grid of context tokens. A second post-encoder stage spends the remaining budget. Training-free. Claims reduced computation of both encoder and LLM but reports only total FLOPs/latency (no encoder-only ms, no description of how the reduced encoder input is shaped). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827)
  - OpenVLA-OFT LIBERO: 50% retention SR 96.5 -> 96.9, 1.33x; 25%: 96.1, 1.61x (FLOPs 4.739 -> 1.921 T); 12.5%: 88.6, 1.66x. pi0.5 LIBERO: 50%: 96.6 -> 96.4 but only 1.02x; 25%: 91.8; 12.5%: 74.0. pi0.5 VLABench: 42.0 -> 26.3 at 50%, 13.7 at 25% (DivPrune and VLA-Pruner score higher at 50%). Real Franka with pi0.5: 60 -> 50% average, latency 174.8 -> 166.4 ms. Jetson not used. [P]
  - Stated limitations: patches removed before the encoder get no global context and cannot be recovered; assumes calibrated cameras and depth, "RGB-only settings are left to future work"; the gripper filling the wrist view is conservatively kept; on pi0.5 a 38% FLOP cut gives ~1.03x because the action expert dominates. [P]
- **Look, Focus, Act** (arXiv 2507.15833, 21 Jul 2025, v2 22 Sep 2025; Chuang et al.). Foveated tokenization (dense small patches at the predicted gaze, coarse rings in the periphery, all resized to the central patch size) feeding a standard ViT-B: 324 -> 20 tokens (-94% tokens and ViT compute); ViT-only 243.8 -> 16.4 ms and 1905.4 -> 115.6 GFLOPs (batch 64); inference per chunk 334.7 -> 87.9 ms (~3.8x); training ~7x faster. Requires: ViT pretrained from scratch with MAE (public DINOv2-style weights assume uniform tokenization and were not used), eye-tracked human demonstrations for gaze supervision; policy is a flow-matching visuomotor policy *without language* (not a VLA). Results on par with baselines, more robust to distractors; real-robot results mixed (Toothbrush: fine 24% vs fov 18%). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15833v2)

**B. Inside-encoder merging/pruning (ViT forward shortened) — mostly negative or VLM-only evidence**
- DepthCache's experiments report that **ToSA** (merging inside the ViT) applied to pi0.5 lost 24.1 success points with a 0.94x "speedup" (slower), and argue ToMe/ToSA "require architecture-specific encoder changes and use uniform merge ratios". [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10469)
- **VScan** (arXiv 2505.22654, TMLR Jan 2026). Training-free two-stage VLM token reduction: stage 1 "during visual encoding" combines a global scan (CLS attention at the output layer) and a local scan (attention at a shallow layer, l=6 for LLaVA, l=8 for Qwen-2.5-VL), merging unselected tokens; stage 2 prunes in the LLM. 1.77x / 2.91x prefill speedup on LLaVA-1.5 / LLaVA-NeXT at 11% retention with 96.7% / 95.4% performance. No encoder-side time is reported separately; SigLIP is not evaluated; for CLS-less encoders it falls back to average received self-attention. Not a robotics paper. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22654v3)
- **Feather the Throttle** (arXiv 2412.13180, ICCV 2025): early token pruning in VLMs gives selection biased toward bottom-of-image tokens. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13180)

**C. Post-encoder pruning (ViT forward NOT shortened)**
- **LightVLA** (arXiv 2509.12594, 16 Sep 2025). Differentiable (Gumbel-softmax) token selection on projected tokens after the encoder+projector; requires fine-tuning (OpenVLA-OFT, 40k steps, LoRA r=32, 8x H20 GPUs). Keeps ~78 of 512 tokens; FLOPs -59.1%, latency 34 -> 21 ms (-38.2%), LIBERO SR 94.8 -> 97.4; simulation only; latency comparisons across methods used different GPUs. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12594v2)
- **DepthCache** (arXiv 2603.10469, Mar 2026). "The entire framework operates outside the vision encoder"; training-free depth-guided merging with a wrist-camera gating state machine; needs depth. pi0.5 1.28x (-0.3 SR), OpenVLA 1.21x (-1.0), GR00T 1.07x (-0.2) on LIBERO, RTX 4090; real pi0.5 191 -> 143 ms. Reports FastV losing 20.3 points on pi0.5 and 12.7 on OpenVLA. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10469)
- **T3R** (arXiv 2611.08413v1, page dated 5 Nov 2026; submitted to ASP-DAC '27). Training-free: SigLIP text-patch similarity + EfficientTAM-S mask (updated asynchronously every 5 VLA calls) prunes patches "before decoder entry", plus attention biasing. OpenVLA-OFT, LIBERO-Spatial: 85% scene-camera token reduction at 96% SR (baseline 97%), 107.5 -> 92.5 ms per call (L40S). Key detail: *retained patches must keep their original RoPE position IDs; re-indexing collapsed success.* Uses a fixed 48.6 ms non-LLM cost in projections; whether the main encoder pass is shortened is not stated. Simulation only, no edge device. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08413)
- **BFA++** (arXiv 2602.20566, 24 Feb 2026). Trained intra-view and inter-view importance predictors (pi0 fully fine-tuned 5,000 steps; RDT 1,200 steps; 8x A100); prunes before the LLM (pi0) or at DiT layer 2 (RDT). pi0 6.5 -> 10.3 Hz, RDT 1.0 -> 1.5 Hz on RTX 3090; SR +~10 points on RoboTwin. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20566v1)
- Other post-encoder or decoder-side VLA pruners seen only in search results: **VLA-ACL** (arXiv 2610.08133; patch-scoring network, fixed per-view budgets), **APS-VLA** (arXiv 2611.13030; learned selector with frozen backbone, budgets 256/128/64), **Token Expand-Merge** (arXiv 2512.09927; training-free), **VLA-IAP** (arXiv 2603.22991; training-free), **Action-JND** (arXiv 2608.21247; a criterion for when pruning or stale-KV reuse is "safe"). [S] — [VLA-ACL](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08133), [APS-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13030), [Token Expand-Merge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.09927), [VLA-IAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22991v1), [Action-JND](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21247)

**D. Foveation / ROI in VLAs (adds or re-allocates pixels; not shown to save compute)**
- **FoveaVLA** (arXiv 2610.20064, 2026; Stanford/Princeton). On pi0.5: the LM head predicts a "foveation action" (four location tokens) per chunk; the next query crops that box from the original-resolution external image (1280x720 on DROID) and feeds it alongside the 224x224 resized full view; replaces the wrist camera. Requires training (RoboInter box annotations; loss L_flow + 0.1 L_foveation). FoveaVLA-Single reaches 70.8% across four real suites, above wrist-equipped pi0.5. The page does not present it as a compute saving. [P, project page] — [FoveaVLA](https://tml.stanford.edu/FoveaVLA/)
- **ROI-Driven Foveated Attention** (arXiv 2603.20668, 21 Mar 2026). Proposal only: project forward-kinematics end-effector poses into a calibrated external camera, crop hand-centred ROIs at 256x256, feed alongside the downsampled global frame (token count triples). No experiments: "dedicated empirical validation remains future work". [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20668)
- **Gaze-Regularized VLAs** (arXiv 2603.23202): feeding a foveated image directly into the encoder reduced LIBERO-Spatial success 85.9% -> 78.5% (attributed to lost peripheral context). [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23202)
- **GazeVLM** (arXiv 2509.16476): training-free VLM method pairing gaze-driven ROI crops with a low-resolution global view to cut vision tokens; not a robot policy. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16476)
- **"See Less, Specify More"** (arXiv 2606.02735v2, 8 Jun 2026; AIRoA): trains the executor under an explicit learned "visual evidence budget" for generalization (pi0.5 mean subtask success 54.2 -> 79.0 on eight real tasks); the abstract frames it as a generalization interface, "beyond efficiency considerations". [P-text, abstract only] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02735)

### Inferences
- Summary of "needs retraining?": training-free = VLA-Cache, TTF-VLA, SpecPrune-VLA, VLA-Pruner, EfficientVLA, FlashVLA, DepthCache, T3R, ActPrune, VScan, STC-Cacher; small auxiliary module trained with frozen VLA = LAC, ElegantVLA (scheduler), APS-VLA; fine-tuning of the VLA = LightVLA, BFA++, FoveaVLA; from-scratch = Look, Focus, Act.
- For SmolVLA-class models, post-encoder pruning has almost nothing left to save (64 tokens/camera already); only pre-encoder or in-encoder reduction touches the ~260 ms. Among those, only ActPrune exists for VLAs, and it has not been tried with a pixel-shuffle connector, where dropped patches must be aligned to the 4x4 patch blocks that are folded into one output token (inference from the 1024 -> 64 arithmetic, not from a source).
- T3R's finding that re-indexing position IDs collapses success, and ActPrune's VLABench collapse, both indicate that pre-/in-encoder patch removal is fragile with respect to positional layout and missing global context.

### Gaps
- No encoder-only latency for any pre-encoder VLA pruning method (ActPrune reports totals only).
- No VLA paper found applying ToMe or FastV-style pruning inside SigLIP with positive results; the only data point is DepthCache's negative ToSA result. Queries tried: "token pruning inside SigLIP vision encoder VLA early layers prune patches robot manipulation training-free"; "VLM token reduction during visual encoding inside ViT VScan SAINT ToMe SigLIP training-free encoder-side speedup".
- No compute-saving foveated tokenization on a pretrained SigLIP inside a VLA. Query tried: "foveated VLA gaze region-of-interest crop high resolution vision-language-action active vision tokens".

## 3. Resolution: multi-/any-resolution training for VLAs, resolution-elastic ViTs, SigLIP position-embedding issues, run-time resolution selection, "freshness vs fidelity"

### Takeaway
No VLA paper was found that trains one checkpoint to work at several input resolutions or that selects resolution at run time; resolution appears in VLA papers only as a fixed design choice or a per-resolution ablation. The enabling vision-side techniques exist (FlexiViT, ResFormer, NaViT, SigLIP 2 NaFlex, Matryoshka query tokens) but have not been carried into robot policies, and the one measured scaling law for a 450M VLA says prefix latency is proportional to megapixels, so resolution is the dominant lever on the vision side.

### Cited Findings
- **SmolVLA** (arXiv 2506.01844). "Training is performed after resizing the images to 512×512, for consistency with the VLM input size"; tiling is skipped and pixel shuffle limits "the visual tokens to 64 per frame"; only the first 16 LLM layers are used (LIBERO avg 75.0 / 78.5 / 79.5 / 80.3 for N = 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 layers); "unused views were dropped during training". The paper has **no ablation on input resolution or visual-token count**, and reports no per-inference latency. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)
- **Measured scaling law for a 450M flow-matching VLA** ("Where Edge VLA Latency Actually Goes", NeurIPS 2026): pre-fill latency ~= 23 ms + 464 ms per megapixel, tracking "encoder pixel work (number of images × resolution) rather than the number of visual tokens"; a two-parameter fit on two settings predicted two held-out settings within 0.1% and 7.3%; batching headroom follows the same quantity ("three cameras at 256 px batch better than one at 512 px"). T4 and AGX Orin are cited; which device the constants refer to is not clear from the page. [P, conference abstract page] — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170035)
- **OpenVLA** (arXiv 2406.09246): trained separately at 224 px and 384 px, "no performance difference in our evaluations, while the latter takes 3x longer to train". This is train-at-each-resolution, not one checkpoint across resolutions. [S] — [OpenVLA paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09246)
- **SPEAR-1** (arXiv 2511.17411): ablation with 280x210 vs 224x224 inputs gave comparable average success on SIMPLER WidowX. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17411)
- **T3R** profiles resolution scaling from 384 to 768 px for OpenVLA-OFT (LLM-forward speedup up to 3.3x at 768 px; *projected*, not measured, 2.47x end-to-end) — a latency projection, not a multi-resolution policy. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08413)
- **EdgeVLA-Base (FMB)** model card: lowers SmolVLA-style input to 256x256 (three cameras, 64 tokens) but by retraining the whole policy with a different encoder. [P, model card] — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/enfuse/edgevla-base-fmb)
- **FlexiViT** (arXiv 2212.08013, CVPR 2023): randomizes patch size during training and resizes patch- and position-embedding weights, so a single set of weights works across sequence lengths; standard ViTs "rapidly degrade as the inference-time patch size departs from the one used during training". [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08013v1)
- **NaViT** (arXiv 2307.06304): native-resolution/aspect-ratio packing with smooth cost-performance trade-off at inference. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06304)
- **ResFormer** (arXiv 2212.00776): ViT performance drops sharply at unseen test resolutions; multi-resolution training with a scale-consistency (self-distillation) loss and global-local position embedding fixes it (ResFormer-S-MR trained on 128/160/224 reaches 82.16% top-1, +2.24 over DeiT-S at 224). Bicubic position-embedding interpolation is the baseline it improves on. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00776)
- **RoPE for ViT** (arXiv 2403.13298): RoPE-Mixed beats ResFormer for extrapolation (res > 224) but is worse for strong downscaling (res <= 160) unless absolute position embeddings are added. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13298)
- **SigLIP 2** (arXiv 2502.14786): the NaFlex variant supports multiple resolutions and native aspect ratio with one checkpoint; the authors note that simply fine-tuning the final checkpoint at a smaller learning rate "did not lead to good results across all sizes and resolutions". [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14786v1)
- **Matryoshka Query Transformer** (arXiv 2405.19315, NeurIPS 2024): a VLM trained with nested query tokens can emit any number of visual tokens at inference; a training-time design, not applicable to an existing checkpoint without training. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19315v1)
- Dynamic compute in VLAs exists on other axes: **DeeR-VLA** (arXiv 2411.02359) early-exits the MLLM; **Decoupled Early Exits** (arXiv 2609.29382) exposes backbone depth, action-expert depth and denoising steps as compute axes (79.2% latency cut per its abstract) — neither varies resolution or touches the vision encoder. [S] — [DeeR-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02359v1), [Decoupled Early Exits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29382v2)

### Inferences
- Plugging the project's configuration into the published law (2 cameras x 512^2 = 0.524 MP) gives 23 + 464 x 0.524 ~= 266 ms, close to the project's measured ~260 ms on Orin Nano; the same law would give ~160 ms at 384 px and ~118 ms at 320 px. This is arithmetic on a law whose reference hardware is not confirmed, so treat the agreement as suggestive only.
- With a fixed patch size and a pixel-shuffle connector, lowering resolution changes not only the position-embedding grid but also the number of tokens the LLM receives (512 px -> 8x8 = 64 tokens; 384 px -> 6x6 = 36; 320 px -> 5x5 = 25, assuming patch 16 and shuffle ratio 4). That is a distribution shift for the connector, LLM prefix and action-expert cross-attention all at once, which plausibly explains why remapping position ids alone did not rescue the project's 384/320-px runs. A FlexiViT-style larger patch that preserves the 32x32 grid would keep the token layout but would not reduce ViT token count, i.e. would not save compute. (Inference from arithmetic; no source tests this.)
- The literature's "freshness vs fidelity" evidence is indirect: the NeurIPS 2026 paper compares stale-prefix reuse against INT8 under motion, but nobody has reported a closed-loop sweep trading resolution (fidelity) against observation age (freshness) for a single VLA.

### Gaps
- No VLA with one checkpoint validated at multiple resolutions, and no run-time resolution selection for a robot policy. Queries tried: "VLA multi-resolution training any-resolution vision-language-action dynamic resolution inference robot"; "VLA input resolution robustness fine-tune multiple resolutions one checkpoint FlexiViT SigLIP position embedding interpolation robot policy"; "\"vision-language-action\" \"native resolution\" OR \"any-resolution\" OR \"multi-resolution\" OR \"resolution-adaptive\" OR \"dynamic resolution\" robot policy inference speed 2026"; "Matryoshka OR elastic visual tokens VLA robot policy variable token budget single checkpoint inference-time resolution lower without retraining success drop"; "resolution randomization fine-tuning robot policy ViT FlexiViT variable patch size manipulation inference cheaper low resolution observation"; "SmolVLA OR pi0 OR OpenVLA image resolution ablation inference lower resolution than training success rate drop LIBERO".
- No published number for the success drop when a VLA is run below its training resolution; the project's -28/-50 points appear to be a new data point.
- No source specifically on SigLIP (v1) learned-position-embedding interpolation failure at reduced resolution was retrieved; the ResFormer/FlexiViT statements are about generic ViTs.

## 4. Smaller or distilled vision encoders, dual-encoder (fast/slow) designs, and vision-encoder quantization on Jetson

### Takeaway
Compact encoders appear in VLAs only via retraining the whole policy (DynamicVLA and EdgeVLA-Base with FastViT; AsyncVLA with EfficientNet-B0 at 96x96; A2C2's ResNet-18 correction head), and no drop-in distilled replacement for a frozen VLA's SigLIP was found. On Jetson, FP16 with careful kernels is the reported working point; INT8 on SigLIP has documented pitfalls (2.4–4.7x *slower* with BitsAndBytes on Orin, activation outliers, TensorRT silently falling back to FP16), and the literature disagrees on how quantization-sensitive the vision encoder is.

### Cited Findings

**A. Compact encoders and fast/slow designs**
- **"Leap-VLA ~23M encoder": not found.** The only "LEAP-VLA" located is a MERL / ECCV 2026 paper on action-latent prototyping (Multi-level Soft Residual Quantization + a lightweight aligner outside the VLM), which is about the action side, with no fast visual stream described. [S] — [MERL TR2026-127](https://shadow.merl.com/publications/TR2026-127), [HF model page](https://huggingface.co/Boyun7/LEAP-VLA)
- **A2C2, "Leave No Observation Behind"** (arXiv 2509.23224, 27 Sep 2025). Per-step correction head on top of a chunking VLA. On LIBERO it uses its *own* ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 on top and wrist images (512-d tokens) plus SmolVLM language embeddings and base-policy latents, a 6-layer transformer encoder and a 3-layer MLP; ~32M parameters; 4.7 ms per step vs 101 ms for SmolVLA on an RTX 5080 laptop GPU. LIBERO-Spatial 81.8 -> 89.2 (e=10, d=0), 64.4 -> 84.2 (e=40, d=10). Simulation only; training requires running the base policy over the dataset; head trained 200k steps on an H200 (~4 h). Internal inconsistencies noted (abstract's "+7 over RTC on LIBERO" has no supporting LIBERO RTC result). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23224v1)
- **HiRT** (arXiv 2410.05273, CoRL 2024). Low-frequency VLM + high-frequency vision-based policy guided by slowly updated VLM features; doubles control frequency on static tasks at comparable success; dynamic real tasks 48% -> 75%. The fast module's encoder is not named on the abstract page. [P, abstract only] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05273)
- **Fast-in-Slow (FiS-VLA)** (arXiv 2506.01953, NeurIPS 2025): System 1 reuses the last transformer blocks of the VLM; vision is SigLIP + DINOv2 (large encoders), 117.7 Hz with chunk size 8. Not a small-encoder design. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01953v1)
- **AsyncVLA** (arXiv 2602.13476; navigation): onboard 76M-parameter fast policy with EfficientNet-B0 at 96x96, remote large model. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13476)
- **Libra-VLA** (arXiv 2604.24921, Apr 2026): asynchronous coarse-to-fine dual system whose System 1 has "an independent high-resolution visual encoder"; baseline inference 220 ms on RTX 4090. Encoder size not extracted. [P-text, partial] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24921)
- **DuoCore-FS** (arXiv 2512.20188): fully asynchronous fast/slow pathways with a latent buffer refreshed by the slow system. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20188)
- **DynamicVLA** (arXiv 2601.22153, 29 Jan 2026; NTU S-Lab): 0.4B VLA with a convolutional FastViT encoder over concatenated multi-frame RGB, plus Continuous Inference and Latent-aware Action Streaming; trained on a 200K-episode synthetic + 2K real dynamic-manipulation benchmark. Latency numbers not retrieved. [S] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22153)
- **EdgeVLA-Base (FMB)** (community model card, enfuse): SmolVLA (450M) with the frozen SigLIP (98M) swapped for a trainable FastViT-sa12 (~12M); 363M total; three cameras at 256x256 -> 64 tokens; trained 150K steps on one H200 (~16 h) on `lerobot/fmb` only. Offline metrics only (action MSE 0.458 vs 0.618 for SmolVLA); latency on H200 FP32 162 ms vs 169 ms (essentially no gain there); the ~368 ms Jetson AGX Orin figure is an estimate, not measured; no closed-loop success reported. [P, model card] — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/enfuse/edgevla-base-fmb)
- **Encoder distillation outside VLAs**: Theia distils several vision foundation models into ViT-Tiny/Small backbones for robot learning; UltraViT (arXiv 2607.23373) is a latency-optimized on-device VLM encoder trained with dense patch-wise cosine distillation; eVGGT (arXiv 2509.15880) is a distilled geometry-aware encoder for visuomotor policies (~5x faster, -63% memory). None was reported as a drop-in replacement validated with a frozen VLA policy. [S] — [Theia](https://rai-inst.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Theia_Distilling-Diverse-Vision-Foundation-Models-for-Robot-Learning.pdf), [UltraViT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23373), [eVGGT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15880)
- Survey-level mentions: Tiny-VLA with FastViT; MiniVLA (HF) keeps DINOv2/SigLIP and accelerates via ONNX/TensorRT with a reported 5–10% LIBERO success drop. [S] — [MiniVLA](https://huggingface.co/xintaozhen/MiniVLA)

**B. Vision-encoder quantization and Jetson deployment**
- **"Rethinking Small VLM Quantization"** (arXiv 2607.08029, 9 Jul 2026; ETRI). Jetson Orin NX 16 GB and AGX Orin 64 GB (Orin Nano excluded: out of memory). Vision-encoder latency FP16 -> INT8 (BitsAndBytes) got *worse*: PaliGemma2 (SigLIP So400m) 311.9 -> 1,203.5 ms on NX (3.86x) and 98.5 -> 459.1 ms on AGX (4.66x); LLaVA-OV-0.5B 719.8 -> 1,879.1 ms (2.61x); Qwen3-VL-2B (SigLIP-2) 1.73x / 2.51x; non-SigLIP Kosmos-2.5 only 1.15–1.20x. Attributed to the BitsAndBytes INT8 kernel path on Ampere, not to SigLIP or INT8 in general. Accuracy impact of vision INT8 was small (-2.39% to +0.63% MME), but vision INT8 + LLM INT4 was non-additive (PaliGemma2: extra 16.33-point drop). [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08029v1)
- **"Activation Quantization of Vision Encoders Needs Prefixing Registers"** (arXiv 2510.04547): SigLIP-B/16 INT8 latency 128.49 -> 62.29 (2.06x); accuracy 76.05 FP -> 69.71 with activation outliers present vs 74.54 with outliers removed; SigLIP2-B/16 78.47 -> 26.04 with outliers vs 74.54 without. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04547v5)
- **VLA quantization sensitivity is contested**: HBVLA (arXiv 2602.13710) reports the vision encoder is considerably sensitive; QVLA (arXiv 2602.03782, ICLR 2026) reports projectors and action heads are far more sensitive than vision encoders; VLAQuantBench (arXiv 2609.25376) reports weight-only W8 tracks LIBERO baselines, activation quantization produces larger differences, and component-level sensitivity ordering reverses between models. [S] — [HBVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13710), [QVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03782v1), [VLAQuantBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25376)
- **vla.cpp** (arXiv 2606.08094, 6 Jun 2026): ggml-class C++ runtime for flow-matching/diffusion VLAs, tested from RTX 3060 to an 8 GB Jetson Orin-class module; explicitly warns that "reduced precision in vision encoders can shift actions" and recommends parity-gated builds. [P, secondary page of the arXiv abstract] — [Emergent Mind page](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.08094)
- **TensorRT INT8 fallback**: in a Jetson study of quantized ACT (arXiv 2608.03938), the ResNet-18 backbone was quantized but none of the 145 transformer encoder/decoder layers were accepted for INT8 and fell back to FP16. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03938)
- **Community Orin Nano 8 GB benchmark** (GitHub `eetmie/jetson-orin-nano-vla`): SmolVLA with 2 cameras at p50 107.55 ms / 9.30 Hz / 1.78 GB RAM (JetPack 7.2.1, MAXN_SUPER, synthetic observations, mostly-FP16 exported engines, action cosine >= 0.99999 vs stock policy); single-view "almost 12 Hz". Optimization log: Triton flash attention replacing TensorRT MHA on the vision path (0.71 -> 0.48 ms per call), patchify+MatMul replacing the conv patch embedding (-0.8 ms), FP32 accumulation of FP16 GEMMs made default (+2.3% latency, mean error -31%; the K=12288 connector is flagged as the worst FP16-accumulation spot), FP32 attention scores in the vision kernel rejected (~9% worse error); components reported: denoise 37.6 ms, prefill 9.6 ms, preprocessing 0.5 ms, total 126.8 -> 104.6 ms. INT8, resolution reduction, camera batching and token reduction were *not* tried. [P, repo README and docs] — [repo](https://github.com/eetmie/jetson-orin-nano-vla), [optimization notes](https://github.com/eetmie/jetson-orin-nano-vla/blob/main/docs/optimization.md)
- **NVIDIA developer forum measurements**: SmolVLA on AGX Thor 197 ms eager -> 50 ms with torch.compile (BF16); no Orin Nano numbers and no component breakdown published there; TensorRT "not measured yet". [P] — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/vla-models-0-5-gr00t-smolvla-measured-on-jetson-agx-thor-agx-orin-and-orin-nano-latency-energy-memory-per-policy-call/387191)
- A blog claims SmolVLA FP16 on ORT-TRT reaches "~25 ms p50, ~20 ms with INT8" on Orin Nano but gives no methodology, camera count or resolution; this conflicts with the ~105 ms measured in the repo above and should be treated as unverified. [P, blog] — [jared-hpc post](https://jared-hpc.com/posts/vla-physical-ai-tensorrt-tether/); contradicted by [repo](https://github.com/eetmie/jetson-orin-nano-vla)

**C. Is the vision encoder really the bottleneck? (context that other papers argue the opposite)**
- NeurIPS 2026 scaling-law paper: for a 450M flow-matching VLA the action expert, not pre-fill, consumes 67% of the single-stream budget, contradicting reports that pre-fill is >90% of forward time. [P] — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170035)
- "Characterizing VLA Models" (arXiv 2603.02271, Google/Purdue, MolmoAct-7B on AGX Orin and Thor): autoregressive generation is ~75% of step latency and memory-bandwidth bound; Thor's ~5x compute yields only ~1.4x end-to-end. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02271v1)
- 2607.12287's Table III: for pi0.5 the ViT is 40.1 of 293.2 ms. [P-text] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12287)

### Inferences
- These "vision is not the bottleneck" results are for uncompiled action experts or 3–7B models; they are consistent with the project's premise that vision becomes the remaining bottleneck *after* the action expert is compiled, and the NeurIPS 2026 megapixel law supports that the remaining cost scales with pixels.
- In the community Orin Nano pipeline, the published components (denoise 37.6 + prefill 9.6 + preprocessing 0.5) leave roughly 57 ms of the 104.6 ms total for everything else including two-camera vision encoding. If the resolution there is the default 512 px, then kernel-level engineering (flash-attention plugin, FP16 TensorRT) may already put two-camera SigLIP well below the project's ~260 ms. The repo does not state the vision time or resolution, so this is an inference that needs checking before attributing the 260 ms to the architecture rather than to the runtime.
- A2C2's design (a cheap ResNet-18 looking at every frame while the VLA runs slowly) is the closest published instance of "compact fast visual stream next to a slow semantic stream" for SmolVLA specifically; it requires training a 32M head.

### Gaps
- Leap-VLA with a ~23M fast encoder: not found. Queries tried: "Leap-VLA lightweight fast visual encoder asynchronous VLA arXiv"; "\"Leap-VLA\" OR \"LeapVLA\" vision-language-action 23M encoder fast stream"; "asynchronous dual-system VLA fast visual stream small encoder ~23M parameters DINOv2-small OR ViT-S high-frequency slow semantic stream stale features 2026".
- No drop-in distilled student for a frozen VLA's SigLIP. Queries tried: "distill SigLIP vision encoder into smaller student for VLA feature distillation frozen policy drop-in replacement robot edge"; "VLA lightweight vision encoder replace SigLIP with FastViT MobileCLIP EfficientViT TinyCLIP distilled encoder edge robot" (no VLA using MobileCLIP, EfficientViT or TinyCLIP surfaced).
- No vision-encoder layer truncation / early exit for VLAs. Query tried: "VLA vision encoder layer skipping early exit ViT depth pruning vision encoder layers truncated robot policy latency" (hits were LLM/action-expert exits: DySL-VLA arXiv 2602.22896, Decoupled Early Exits arXiv 2609.29382, MoLe-VLA arXiv 2503.20384).
- No measured TensorRT INT8 result for SigLIP inside a VLA on Jetson with closed-loop success. Query tried: "VLA quantization vision encoder sensitivity INT8 SigLIP TensorRT Jetson pitfalls QVLA".
- HiRT's and Libra-VLA's fast-encoder identities and sizes were not extracted.

## 5. Multi-camera handling: batching vs sequential, per-camera token budgets, camera dropout, view selection, different resolution or rate per camera

### Takeaway
Multi-camera work in VLAs is about *token budgets and view importance after encoding* (BFA++, VLA-ACL, APS-VLA, T3R, DepthCache's wrist gate, ActPrune's asymmetric treatment) or about camera-count flexibility (VersaCamVLA, SmolVLA's dropped views); no paper was found that gives cameras different encoder refresh rates or different resolutions (e.g. wrist every step at high resolution, third-person less often or coarser) and evaluates it closed-loop.

### Cited Findings
- **Batching and pixel work**: prefix latency and batching headroom both follow megapixels; "three cameras at 256 px batch better than one at 512 px" (450M VLA; NeurIPS 2026). [P] — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170035)
- **Parallel encoding across devices** (vla.cpp PR #59, Intel Core Ultra 7 356H, 512 px, SmolVLA): GPU ~22 ms/view, NPU ~31 ms/view. Offloading one view's SigLIP to the NPU: 3 views, vision 66 -> 50 ms, request 151.5 -> 135.5 ms; 2 views, vision 44 -> 39 ms but request 120.8 -> 121.5 ms (no net gain; shared-memory contention slowed the GPU 5–25%). LIBERO-Object 87/100 vs 80/100 (within noise, p ~= 0.2); action difference <= 2.2e-3. Calibrated INT8 on the NPU is named as the next lever (estimated ~22 ms/view), not implemented. Not Jetson. [P] — [vla.cpp PR #59](https://github.com/VinRobotics/vla.cpp/pull/59)
- **Camera count vs speed on Orin Nano**: SmolVLA 2 cameras 9.30 Hz vs single view "almost 12 Hz" (experimental custom checkpoint). [P] — [repo](https://github.com/eetmie/jetson-orin-nano-vla)
- **View importance varies by phase** (BFA++): "the head view suffices during approach and post-manipulation, while the wrist view is critical during manipulation"; a learned inter-view predictor weights views from their CLS tokens; pruning is post-encoder and needs fine-tuning. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20566v1)
- **Wrist-camera gating** (DepthCache): a two-state machine gates wrist-camera token compression based on gripper and end-effector motion; post-encoder, training-free. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10469)
- **Asymmetric per-view pre-encoder pruning** (ActPrune): wrist view pruned by motion-compensated reprojection (needs depth), third-person by projected trajectory. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827)
- **Multi-camera headroom** (T3R): argues multi-camera setups have more pruning headroom because views overlap; 85% scene-camera reduction on OpenVLA-OFT; on pi0.5/RoboTwin with three cameras 38.3% at 62.5% keep vs 33.3% baseline. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08413)
- **Fixed per-view budgets**: VLA-ACL applies a fixed per-view token budget (two wrist + one static camera); APS-VLA solves the budget separately per view and lists adaptive cross-camera allocation as future work. [S] — [VLA-ACL](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08133), [APS-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13030)
- **Camera-count flexibility / dropout**: VersaCamVLA (arXiv 2610.12451, 8 Oct 2026; pi0.5) maps any set of *posed* views to a fixed number of scene tokens, trained with random static-view subsets and wrist views included with probability 0.5; one checkpoint evaluated at 3–6 views; requires calibration; resolution variation not discussed. SmolVLA drops unused views during pretraining. [P] — [VersaCamVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12451), [SmolVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)
- **View selection by entropy**: UniviewVLA (arXiv 2606.21501) generates auxiliary views compressed to few tokens and selects the most action-informative view by action entropy. [S] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21501)
- **Sensitivity to stale or missing views**: a community repo (`chaoshengsc/libero-camera-ablation`) reports on LIBERO-Spatial that OpenVLA and SmolVLA fall to 0% with black images, and LeRobot's public pi0 checkpoint goes 76% -> 56% with black cameras and 76% -> 71% with a *frozen* image. CoRe-VLA (arXiv 2609.37150) reports over-reliance on wrist-view cues when the global view is lost. [S] — [repo](https://github.com/chaoshengsc/libero-camera-ablation), [CoRe-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37150v1)
- **Replacing the wrist camera by a crop**: FoveaVLA (see section 2). [P] — [FoveaVLA](https://tml.stanford.edu/FoveaVLA/)

### Inferences
- The pieces for a mixed-rate scheme exist separately — phase-dependent view importance (BFA++), a hand-written wrist gate (DepthCache), a 5-point drop for pi0 with a completely frozen image (community repo), and megapixel-proportional cost (NeurIPS 2026) — but nobody has combined them into "refresh camera A every step, camera B every k steps" for the encoder itself.
- Because cost is proportional to megapixels, halving the third-person camera's refresh rate or its side length is worth more than any post-encoder token trick for a two-camera SmolVLA; but run-below-training-resolution fails without retraining (project's measurement), so the training-free option is rate, not resolution.

### Gaps
- No paper on per-camera encoder refresh rates or per-camera resolution in VLAs. Queries tried: "VLA multi-camera view selection token budget wrist camera lower resolution per-view pruning efficient"; "VLA cameras different update rates wrist camera high frequency third-person low frequency asynchronous multi-camera encoding stale view"; "VLA \"third-person\" camera features cached reused while wrist camera re-encoded every step selective per-view vision encoder refresh policy".
- No Jetson measurement comparing batched vs sequential two-camera SigLIP encoding was found (the community repo did not test it).
- The frozen-frame numbers come from a GitHub README seen only in search results; not verified.

## 6. Event cameras / event-driven perception for VLAs: compute savings or robustness?

### Takeaway
The event-camera VLA work found (E-VLA) is about robustness in dark and motion-blurred scenes and adds compute or keeps it constant; no event-driven VLA was found that uses event sparsity to skip or shorten vision-encoder computation. "EventVLA" is an unrelated memory method that does not use event cameras.

### Cited Findings
- **E-VLA: Event-Augmented VLA for Dark and Blurred Scenes** (arXiv 2604.04834, 6 Apr 2026; Zhai, Shi, Guo, Yang, Wang). Base: SmolVLA (~0.5B, SigLIP, frozen VLM backbone). DAVIS346 event camera wrist-mounted on an SO100 arm; inference on NVIDIA AGX Orin. Event representation: polarity-agnostic accumulated event-count frames over a "recent-count" window (2000 events best). Fusion: (1) parameter-free overlay of event colours onto RGB before encoding (0 parameters, ~0 extra GFLOPs); (2) hierarchical event adapter (16x16 patch embedding + four Transformer blocks fused into SigLIP at layers 3/6/9/12; +13.3M parameters, +20.4 GFLOPs, <3% of the model). Results: low-light sweep average 47.5% (image) / 80.8% (overlay) / 94.2% (adapter); at 20 lux Pick-Place 0% -> 60% -> 90%; under 1000 ms blur Sorting 5% -> 32.5%. Efficiency claim is only relative to reconstruction baselines (EvLight, E2VID); no inference latency reported; no encoder skipping. Limitations: weak colour semantics, passive perception relying on robot-induced motion, small dataset, noise near 2 lux. [P] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04834v1)
- **EventVLA** (arXiv 2606.20092, Jun 2026): "event-driven visual evidence memory" for long-horizon, non-Markovian VLA tasks; not event cameras. [S] — [Emergent Mind page](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.20092)
- **EP-VLM** (arXiv 2506.07627): uses event-based priors to make VLMs more efficient for edge deployment; a VLM, not a VLA. [S] — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07627)

### Inferences
- E-VLA shows an event stream can be attached to SmolVLA on an Orin, and that event activity varies with robot motion across task phases; using that signal as a *gate* for which RGB patches (or which camera) to re-encode would be a compute-saving use, but no paper does this.
- A cheap frame-difference map from the RGB stream itself is the software analogue (it is what VLA-Cache and TTF-VLA already compute on raw pixels), so the event-camera route is only attractive where exposure or blur makes RGB differencing unreliable.

### Gaps
- No event-driven VLA with measured compute or latency savings, and no spiking VLA, was found. Queries tried: "event camera vision-language-action E-VLA Event-VLA arXiv"; "event camera VLA efficiency sparse events compute savings low latency vision-language-action spiking".
- A paper literally titled "Event-VLA" (hyphenated) distinct from E-VLA and EventVLA was not found.

## 7. Which concrete directions for cheaper/fresher vision in small VLAs appear NOT to have been done yet (training-free or light fine-tuning on an 8 GB GPU)?

### Takeaway
After targeted searches, five directions appear open or only partially explored for SmolVLA-class models: (1) training-free sparse temporal token updates inside SigLIP, per camera, measured on Jetson; (2) per-camera mixed-rate encoder refresh; (3) light multi-resolution fine-tuning for one resolution-elastic checkpoint with run-time resolution choice; (4) RGB-only, proprioception-guided pre-encoder patch dropping / compute-saving foveation; (5) a drop-in distilled vision tower for a frozen policy. In each case the closest prior work targets 3–7B models on desktop GPUs, acts after the encoder, or retrains the whole policy.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence that the encoder is untouched by mainstream VLA acceleration: VLA-Cache, LAC, TTF-VLA, SpecPrune-VLA, VLA-Pruner, LightVLA, DepthCache, BFA++ all operate at or after the encoder output (sections 1–2). — [VLA-Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2), [LAC](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00686), [TTF-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19257v3), [SpecPrune-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05614v2), [VLA-Pruner](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16449v5), [LightVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12594v2), [DepthCache](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10469), [BFA++](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20566v1)
- The two exceptions that do shorten the encoder in a VLA: in-ViT token reuse on pi0/pi0.5 (ViT 40.1 -> 28.5 ms, A100, attention-only, training status unclear) and ActPrune's pre-encoder pruning (needs calibration + depth; no encoder-only timing; RGB-only left to future work). — [2607.12287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12287), [ActPrune](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827)
- Whole-prefix caching was worse than INT8 in all 24 moving conditions for a 450M VLA (open-loop, synthetic stimuli). — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170035)
- STC-Cacher shows training-free in-ViT reuse needs periodic refresh (interval 4; static caching collapses accuracy 43.7 -> 33.5). — [STC-Cacher](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00891)
- Retained patches must keep original position IDs (T3R); patches dropped before encoding lose global context (ActPrune); a foveated image fed directly to the encoder cost 7.4 points on LIBERO-Spatial (Gaze-Regularized VLA, secondary). — [T3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08413), [ActPrune](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827), [Gaze-Regularized VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23202)
- SmolVLA's paper has no resolution or token-count ablation; SigLIP 2 reports that naive low-LR fine-tuning did not give good multi-resolution behaviour; FlexiViT/ResFormer show multi-resolution robustness is obtainable with training. — [SmolVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844), [SigLIP 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14786v1), [FlexiViT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08013v1), [ResFormer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.00776)
- The only SigLIP -> small-encoder swap in a SmolVLA-like model (EdgeVLA-Base) retrained the policy for 150K steps on an H200 and has offline metrics only. — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/enfuse/edgevla-base-fmb)

### Inferences

**Ranked list of open / under-explored directions (at most five). "Open" means: not found after the queries listed under Gaps; it does not prove absence.**

**1. Training-free sparse temporal token update inside SigLIP for a small VLA, per camera, with wall-clock measured on Jetson.**
- Closest prior work: "Reducing Temporal Redundancy for Efficient VLA Inference" (arXiv 2607.12287); STC-Cacher (arXiv 2512.00891); Eventful Transformers (arXiv 2308.13494); Déjà Vu/ReuseViT (arXiv 2506.14107).
- Why it is not the same thing: 2607.12287 is on pi0/pi0.5 at 224 px (256 tokens), skips only attention, reports A100/RTX 4090 PyTorch timings, does not say whether reuse is training-free, and does not treat cameras separately; STC-Cacher/Eventful/Déjà Vu are video-understanding systems never evaluated on a control policy; none handles a pixel-shuffle connector (where a 4x4 patch block feeds one LLM token, so block-level gating is the natural unit) or 1024 patches per camera, where attention is a larger share of the cost.
- Main technical risk: FLOP savings not turning into wall-clock savings on TensorRT/Jetson (static shapes, gather/scatter overhead — Déjà Vu needed stream compaction and notes prior methods lacked GPU-optimized implementations); feature drift between refreshes (STC-Cacher); the wrist camera's ego-motion leaving few static tokens (LAC, ActPrune); and the NeurIPS 2026 result that stale-prefix error compounds under motion.
- Testable without retraining: yes (open-loop feature/action error vs update ratio first, then closed-loop LIBERO).

**2. Per-camera mixed-rate encoder refresh (e.g. wrist camera every policy call, third-person camera every k-th call or only when a cheap pixel-difference / gripper-state trigger fires).**
- Closest prior work: ElegantVLA (arXiv 2605.29438, skips vision + LLM wholesale via a PPO-trained scheduler on GR00T/CogACT); the whole-prefix caching baseline in "Where Edge VLA Latency Actually Goes"; DepthCache's wrist-camera state machine (post-encoder); BFA++'s phase-dependent view importance; VLA-Cache/TTF-VLA raw-pixel static-patch detectors.
- Why it is not the same thing: prior work caches everything or nothing, or re-weights tokens after both cameras have already been encoded; none reuses one camera's encoder output while refreshing the other, and none reports the closed-loop cost of such asymmetry. With megapixel-proportional cost, skipping one of two 512-px encodes would remove roughly half of the vision time on the skipped calls (inference from the scaling law).
- Main technical risk: cross-view temporal inconsistency (the policy was trained on synchronized views); over-reliance on the wrist view when the global view is stale (CoRe-VLA); the frozen-image result (pi0 76 -> 71% on LIBERO-Spatial with a permanently frozen frame, community repo, unverified) suggests moderate but non-zero sensitivity, and the scaling-law paper found stale reuse worse than INT8 under motion.
- Testable without retraining: yes.

**3. Light multi-resolution fine-tuning so one small-VLA checkpoint works at 512 / 384 / 320 px, plus run-time resolution selection (a closed-loop "freshness vs fidelity" study).**
- Closest prior work: FlexiViT (arXiv 2212.08013), ResFormer (arXiv 2212.00776), NaViT (arXiv 2307.06304), SigLIP 2 NaFlex (arXiv 2502.14786) on the vision side; Matryoshka Query Transformer (arXiv 2405.19315) for elastic token counts in VLMs; per-resolution ablations in OpenVLA and SPEAR-1; the megapixel latency law.
- Why it is not the same thing: none is a robot policy; OpenVLA/SPEAR-1 train a separate model per resolution; no VLA paper found trains one checkpoint for several resolutions or switches resolution at run time, and no published number exists for the below-training-resolution failure the project measured (-28 / -50 points).
- Main technical risk: with pixel shuffle the LLM-side token grid changes with resolution (64 -> 36 -> 25 tokens), so the connector, LLM prefix and action-expert cross-attention all need adaptation, not just position embeddings; SigLIP 2's authors report naive low-LR fine-tuning was insufficient for multi-resolution; fitting resolution-randomized fine-tuning (even LoRA/connector-only) of a 450M model at 512 px into 8 GB is unverified.
- Testable without retraining: no (the training-free version is what already failed); needs light fine-tuning. The freshness-vs-fidelity measurement itself becomes possible once such a checkpoint exists.

**4. RGB-only, proprioception/action-guided pre-encoder patch dropping or compute-saving foveation for a small VLA (keep 4x4-block-aligned patches near the projected end-effector path, coarse or cached context elsewhere).**
- Closest prior work: ActPrune (arXiv 2611.08827); Look, Focus, Act (arXiv 2507.15833); ROI-Driven Foveated Attention (arXiv 2603.20668); FoveaVLA (arXiv 2610.20064); GazeVLM (arXiv 2509.16476).
- Why it is not the same thing: ActPrune needs calibrated cameras and wrist depth, was tested on OpenVLA-OFT/pi0.5 with no encoder-only timing, and lists RGB-only as future work; Look, Focus, Act trains a ViT from scratch with human gaze data and no language; ROI-Driven has no experiments and triples tokens; FoveaVLA adds a high-resolution crop (more, not fewer, pixels) and fine-tunes pi0.5.
- Main technical risk: patches removed before encoding lose global context and cannot be recovered (ActPrune; pi0.5 on VLABench 42.0 -> 26.3 at 50% retention); positional layout must be preserved (T3R); feeding a foveated image directly hurt LIBERO-Spatial by 7.4 points (secondary source); end-effector projection still needs at least approximate extrinsics for the third-person camera.
- Testable without retraining: partially — block-aligned patch dropping with original position ids, with dropped blocks filled from the previous frame's cached output tokens, can be tried training-free (this overlaps with direction 1); a two-resolution foveated input almost certainly needs light fine-tuning.

**5. Drop-in distilled small vision tower for a frozen small VLA (student trained to match the teacher's post-pixel-shuffle 64 tokens per camera on robot images), with a parity-gated FP16/INT8 TensorRT study on Jetson.**
- Closest prior work: EdgeVLA-Base (FastViT swap, community model card); DynamicVLA (arXiv 2601.22153); Theia; UltraViT (arXiv 2607.23373); A2C2's ResNet-18 correction head (arXiv 2509.23224); quantization studies (arXiv 2607.08029, 2510.04547, QVLA, HBVLA, VLAQuantBench).
- Why it is not the same thing: EdgeVLA-Base and DynamicVLA retrain the entire policy (150K steps on an H200 for the former, offline metrics only); Theia/UltraViT distil general-purpose encoders and were not validated as replacements under a frozen VLA; A2C2 adds a second stream instead of replacing the first; the quantization papers do not report closed-loop success for SigLIP INT8 under TensorRT on Jetson.
- Main technical risk: small patch-level feature mismatch may cause closed-loop failure even at high cosine similarity (the scaling-law paper shows cosine can hide a 6.9x action-error gap); distillation data coverage; whether a student at 512 px is actually faster on Orin Nano than an optimized FP16 SigLIP (the community pipeline's total is already ~105 ms); INT8 may be slower or fall back to FP16 depending on kernels.
- Testable without retraining: no for the student (needs distillation, plausibly feasible on 8 GB since only the student trains against cached teacher features); yes for the precision/parity study.

**Directions deliberately NOT listed as open** because close prior work exists: LLM-side visual token pruning or KV caching for VLAs (crowded: VLA-Cache, LAC, SpecPrune-VLA, VLA-Pruner, LightVLA, EfficientVLA, FlashVLA, T3R, DepthCache, VLA-ACL, APS-VLA); whole-step skipping of vision + LLM (ElegantVLA, FlashVLA action reuse, SP-VLA scheduling); a lightweight per-step correction head beside a slow VLA (A2C2); replacing the wrist camera with a learned crop (FoveaVLA); event cameras for robustness (E-VLA).
