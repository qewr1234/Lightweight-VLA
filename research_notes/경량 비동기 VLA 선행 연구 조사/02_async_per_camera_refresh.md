# Asynchronous / multi-rate VLA designs and per-camera or change-triggered refresh of cached visual features (state of literature as of 2026-11-16)

Verification legend used throughout:
- **[V-full]** = read from the arXiv full-text HTML (through a page-reading tool that returns extracted answers; quoted fragments are short verbatim fragments returned by that tool, the rest is paraphrase).
- **[V-abs]** = read from the arXiv abstract page or official project/blog page only (no full text).
- **[S]** = known only from a search-engine result summary or a secondary summary; not opened. Treat numbers as unverified.

Candidate idea being checked: "In an asynchronous VLA pipeline (slow vision thread fills a prefix/KV cache; fast action thread generates chunks from the latest cache), refresh the vision cache per camera rather than all cameras together, and decide which camera to re-encode (or whether to re-encode at all) from how much that view changed and how much it matters — an AoII-style index: importance x change-since-last-encode / cost — without retraining the policy."

## 1. What exactly do the named papers do, and what do they leave open?

### Takeaway
Every asynchronous / dual-rate VLA found refreshes its visual-semantic cache for all cameras jointly, on a fixed period or "as soon as the previous slow pass finishes"; none refreshes per camera. Two of the closest papers explicitly name change-triggered refresh as unfinished business: DAM-VLA ("A detector that triggers the VLM on change would close this gap") and Leap-VLA (a trigger path is "supported by the design but not exercised in any reported cell"), and "Acting While Understanding" lists "adaptive semantic refresh" as future work.

### Cited Findings

**DAM-VLA — arXiv 2606.12105, 10 Jun 2026 [V-full]** (Vanjani, Li, Suliga, Reuss, Geraci, Jiang, Lioutikov)
- Cached: one latent buffer per modality (language, vision, proprioception, force/torque). Language encoded once per episode; vision recorded at 25 Hz and the VLM encoder "is invoked every 4 inference steps"; proprio and F/T at 100 Hz, updated every control step; the action head reads all buffers every inference step. A rolling memory of the K latest frame embeddings is compressed to N_mem tokens. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Refresh type: periodic (fixed stride), not content-triggered. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Cameras: "The primary camera and the wrist camera are encoded by a vision-language encoder to generate a sequence of patch tokens"; no separate per-camera buffer or per-camera rate is described. Vision is one modality. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Training: required. Built on X-VLA; vision encoders and action experts fine-tuned, new gated cross-attention modules trained (LR 2e-4, batch 192, 20k steps, NVIDIA GH200 node). Inference on RTX 4060 Ti. No per-stage latency in ms reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Results: 7 real contact-rich tasks, Franka Panda + Robotiq 2F-85 (DROID-style), 15 trials/task. Average success 95.2% vs 40.95% (X-VLA at 25 Hz), 21.9% (X-VLA at 100 Hz), 54.3% (concatenation baseline). 100 Hz control; replanning about 5.5 Hz vs about 1 Hz (X-VLA25). Tracking lag on Sweep 0.116 s vs 0.189 s. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12105), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- No ablation of per-modality or per-camera staleness. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Stated limitations (Section 5): "the camera updates on a fixed timer rather than when the scene changes"; "A detector that triggers the VLM on change would close this gap"; the model "does not use force to correct actions within a chunk"; force comes from Franka joint-torque estimates rather than a dedicated F/T sensor. No mention of age of information or scheduling. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)

**Leap-VLA — arXiv 2611.01011, 31 Oct 2026 [V-full, first 100k chars + appendix tail]** (Yang, Shen, Li, Tian, Wang, Huang, Wang, Zhu, Jiang, Xie, Fang)
- Cached: the VLM's processed scene prefix (instruction + camera observation); the paper says the form is exchangeable (per-layer KV pairs or backbone features). A fast flow-matching expert (1536-wide, 32 blocks) reads the cache plus a fresh visual stream from a compact (~23M-param) encoder pretrained on 62 robot datasets (~319k episodes, ~73M frames). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- Refresh: periodic every T_ref (0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 s tested, against a 0.5 s re-plan clock) plus episode reset. Trigger events (large frame difference / optical flow, gripper open/close, subtask change) are part of the design, but "the trigger path is supported by the design but not exercised in any reported cell." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- Cameras: the fast stream encodes a fresh dual-frame pair per camera (196 tokens per camera, V cameras give V x 196 tokens). No per-camera staleness analysis and no per-camera refresh of the slow cache. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- Training: required. Slow loop Qwen2.5-VL-2B (frozen, LoRA, or full fine-tune depending on task); the expert is conditioned on measured cache age via adaptive layer norm and trained on cache ages sampled from real episode history plus a stale-frame pixel-corruption curriculum. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01011), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- Numbers: LIBERO-Spatial frequency-matched reference degrades 97.5% (0.2 s refresh) to 82.9% (1 s) to 61.4% (2 s); with async timing exposure 92.5% / 91.8%; plus measured-age conditioning 94.5% / 92.9%; plus stale-frame masking curriculum 95.9% / 95.5% (1 s / 2 s). Four-suite LIBERO average 95.2% at 1 s and 94.5% at 2 s refresh vs 96.3% frequency-matched anchor. RTX 4090D action latency 73.9 ms (same-frequency) vs 26.7 ms (decoupled); max re-plan rate 13.5 Hz vs 26 Hz. Real robot: four platforms, seven tasks, 50 trials each. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- Stated limitations: staleness beyond the trained range is not covered; between refreshes the system "does not create information"; evaluation limited to quasi-static simulation and a small real-robot set; a residual chunk-boundary conflict "must be closed in training, not by pushing inference harder." The paper does not use the term "age of information" and does not describe an adaptive scheduler for when to refresh. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)

**D2-VLA — arXiv 2609.34792, 28 Sep 2026 (v2 30 Sep 2026) [V-full incl. appendix]** (Ye, Wei, Huang, Zheng, Zou, Wu, Zhao, Peng, Yang, Zhao, Wang, Qi)
- Cached: block-wise causal KV cache; each observation is one Image-Task block. Slow queue (capacity 4) of full VLM-generated KV blocks; fast queue (capacity 2) of adapter-refined blocks. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)
- Refresh: VLM refresh is periodic every V = 75 environment steps (V a multiple of replanning interval E = 25); between refreshes a gated adapter injects fresh visual features into the latest slow KV block at each replanning step without rerunning the VLM. A normalized elapsed-time value within the refresh cycle is fed to the adapter. No event-triggered refresh. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)
- Cameras: all camera views are concatenated inside one block (three views = 768 visual tokens); the paper notes the block index "identifies the current block, not a camera." The only per-camera element is the historical-token budget: selection is done "separately within each historical block and camera, rather than globally across the entire history" (camera quotas, at least four tokens per camera). Refresh timing is per observation time, not per camera. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)
- Training: required. Adds a full-block visual KV adapter to pi0.5 base (PaliGemma-3B + flow-matching expert), trained end-to-end with the flow-matching loss (75k updates on DOMINO, 50k on DOMINO-Long and real tasks). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)
- Numbers: DOMINO 29.3% SR vs 9.6% (pi0.5) and 17.2% (PUMA); DOMINO-Long 60.0% vs 35.4% and 20.6%; LIBERO-Long 97.5%; RoboTwin 2.0 74.3%; 8 real tasks on Unitree G1D. Latency (GPU not named): LIBERO-Long 174.92 ms vs 141.03 ms for pi0.5; DOMINO 91.43 ms vs 89.62 ms. Update-frequency ablation on DOMINO: no dual-frequency 16.1%, V:E = 50:25 gives 27.6%, V:E = 75:25 gives 29.3%. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34792), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)
- Limitations: no explicit limitations or future-work section was found in the text read. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792)

**TIC-VLA — arXiv 2602.02459, 2 Feb 2026 (v2 29 May 2026) [V-full, first 100k chars]** (Huang, Zhang, Liu, Song, Tang, Ma) — navigation, not manipulation
- Cached: the VLM's last-layer KV cache and reasoning output, with latency metadata (elapsed time and ego-motion offset). Refresh is completion-driven (cache updates when a VLM inference finishes), about 0.5 Hz in simulation; action policy at 10 Hz. Real VLM latency about 3.4 s (RTX 4060), 4.8 s (Orin NX), 1.7 s (A6000). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459)
- Cameras: one front-facing RGB camera on the real robot; no multi-camera question arises. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459)
- Training: required (InternVL3-1B full SFT; action expert; PPO fine-tuning with injected reasoning delays). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459)
- Numbers: DynaNav SR 55.29 (with RL); KV-cache interface with latency-aware training SR 47.06 vs 30.59 without latency awareness; real Unitree Go2 success 0.85 (RTX 4060), 0.75 (Orin NX), 0.80 (A6000). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459)
- Limitations: "the current system is not yet fully optimized for runtime efficiency"; "the real-world evaluation remains limited in scale"; extension to manipulation is future work. No adaptive refresh. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459)

**DuoCore-FS ("Asynchronous Fast-Slow VLA Policies for Whole-Body Robotic Manipulation") — arXiv 2512.20188, 23 Dec 2025 [V-full]** (Zou, Zeng, Nong, Li, Liu, Yang, Ling, Li, Ma; Astribot)
- Cached: a "bridge buffer" holding instruction embeddings and learnable fusion-query embeddings from the slow system, "periodically refreshed"; slow writes 1-3 Hz (Table 1: 3.27 Hz), fast reads 25-30 Hz (32.3 Hz measured on one RTX 4090 with TensorRT, vs 12.5 Hz for pi0). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188)
- Cameras: head + left-hand + right-hand, all 224x224; no per-camera routing or rates. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188)
- Training: required (slow system pi0-FAST on PaliGemma-3B; fast diffusion transformer; joint training with frame offset between systems sampled uniformly from 0-25 frames; 24 H100s for stage 1). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188)
- Numbers: in-distribution 90% (18/20) vs pi0 85%; OOD 50% (5/10) vs 10%; small trial counts. No staleness or delay ablation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188)
- Limitations: "While effective, DuoCore-FS still presents several limitations"; future work includes "Designing more effective and sophisticated bridging mechanisms between the slow and fast pathways." No adaptive or event-triggered refresh. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188)

**AsyncVLA (navigation) — arXiv 2602.13476, 13 Feb 2026 [V-full]** (Hirose, Glossop, Shah, Levine)
- A remote base VLA (OmniVLA, 8.26B) sends compressed action-token embeddings (8 x 1024) with the source-image timestamp over WiFi, up to about 5 Hz on an RTX 4090; an onboard Edge Adapter (~76M params, EfficientNet-B0, 96x96 images) runs about 8 Hz on Jetson Orin and uses whatever embedding arrived last. Training required (two stages, LoRA). Success 0.85 vs 0.45 (OmniVLA). Limitations: "we are still limited to using base VLA that have open-source weights and, therefore, can be finetuned end-to-end"; future work is to "further decouple the base VLA and Edge Adapter policies." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13476)
- Name collision: a different paper, "AsyncVLA: Asynchronous Flow Matching for Vision-Language-Action Models" (arXiv 2511.14148), is about asynchronous denoising of action tokens with a confidence rater, not asynchronous perception. [S] — [arXiv 2511.14148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14148)

**VLA-Cache — arXiv 2502.02175 (NeurIPS 2025) [V-full]**
- Cached: KV states in the language decoder for visually static tokens from the previous frame. Static = pixel-patch cosine similarity above a threshold (0.996 in simulation, 0.85 on the real robot) with a top-k filter (k = 100); tokens with text-to-vision attention above 0.5 are excluded from reuse. Decision recomputed every timestep; no periodic refresh; synchronous. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)
- Cameras: OpenVLA-OFT experiments use third-person + wrist views, but no per-camera reuse statistics are reported and the paper does not say whether wrist tokens are reused less. The paper does not state that the vision encoder is skipped for reused tokens (FLOP accounting covers Transformer decoder layers). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)
- Training-free. Bases: OpenVLA, CogACT, OpenVLA-OFT. LIBERO OpenVLA 75.0% to 74.7% with FLOPs 1.864T to 1.355T, latency 51.91 ms to 31.83 ms (RTX 4090); OpenVLA-OFT 96.8% to 97.4%, 65.10 Hz to 78.98 Hz; real Kinova Jaco2 82.1% to 84.6%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)
- Limitations: with substantial motion "the number of non-reusable tokens increases"; only LLaMA2-based decoders tested, other backbones "remains an open direction for future work." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)

**SmolVLA asynchronous inference stack — arXiv 2506.01844, 2 Jun 2025 [V-full, first 100k chars]**
- Decouples action-chunk prediction (PolicyServer) from execution (RobotClient). A new observation is sent when the remaining queue fraction drops below threshold g (g = 0.7 example); a similarity filter drops near-duplicate observations, and that filter is on joint-space distance (threshold epsilon), not on images. No KV/prefix cache, no per-camera handling, no extra training for async. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)
- SO100 real-world: task time 9.70 s async vs 13.75 s sync; 19 vs 9 pick-place cycles in a fixed time; success 73.3% async vs 78.3% sync. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)

**Helix (Figure) — official blog [V-abs]**
- S2 (7B VLM) at 7-9 Hz, S1 (80M visuomotor transformer) at 200 Hz; S2 runs as an asynchronous background process updating a shared latent vector; a temporal offset between S1 and S2 inputs is added in training to match the deployed latency gap; S1 and S2 each on a dedicated embedded GPU. Trained end-to-end; no per-camera or triggered refresh described. — [Figure Helix page](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix)

**GR00T N1 — arXiv 2503.14734 [S]**
- Secondary summaries report System 2 at 10 Hz (on an L40 GPU) and System 1 action module at 120 Hz, tightly coupled and jointly trained. Not opened in this research. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14734v2)

**HiRT — arXiv 2410.05273, CoRL 2024 [V-abs]**
- Large VLM at "low frequencies" for slowly changing features; a high-frequency vision-based policy conditioned on the slowly updated features. Control frequency doubled on static tasks; dynamic-task success 48% to 75%. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05273)

**Fast-in-Slow (FiS-VLA) — arXiv 2506.01953, 2 Jun 2025 [V-abs + S]**
- System 1 embedded inside the System 2 VLM with shared parameters; "asynchronous operating frequencies"; up to 117.7 Hz with action chunk 8; trained with dual-aware co-training. A 1:4 System-2:System-1 ratio is reported by secondary summaries [S]. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01953)

**OpenHelix — arXiv 2505.03912, 6 May 2025 [V-abs]**
- Survey + empirical analysis + open-source dual-system VLA. Abstract-only reading; no detail on asynchronous refresh obtained. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03912)

**A2C2 ("Leave No Observation Behind") — arXiv 2509.23224, 27 Sep 2025 [S + V-full via comparison paper]**
- Small correction head run every control step on the latest observation, adding a residual to the stale base action; base policy frozen (head is trained). Reported +23 points on Kinetix and +7 points on LIBERO-Spatial over RTC [S]. — [arXiv 2509.23224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23224v1), [Comparison paper 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

**VLASH — arXiv 2512.01031, 30 Nov 2025 [S + V-full via comparison paper]**
- Rolls the proprioceptive state forward through already-committed actions and fine-tunes with temporal-offset augmentation; claims up to 2.03x speedup and up to 17.4x lower reaction latency [S]. Its offset training keeps the visual observation fixed while the state varies. — [arXiv 2512.01031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01031v1), [Comparison paper 2605.08168](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

**"Understanding Asynchronous Inference Methods for VLA Models" — arXiv 2605.08168, 4 May 2026 [V-full]** (Agouzoul)
- Controlled comparison of IT-RTC (training-free), TT-RTC (fine-tuning), VLASH (fine-tuning), A2C2 (trained residual head) on Kinetix and LIBERO (SmolVLA base). LIBERO at delay d = 20: naive ~10-12%, IT-RTC ~20%, TT-RTC ~25-33%, A2C2 ~58%, VLASH ~49-56%. Delay is applied to the whole observation; visual vs proprioceptive vs per-camera staleness is not separated. Limitation: "Our evaluation treats d as a fixed integer; in real deployments d varies with GPU scheduling, network jitter." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168)

**Additional closely related async / cache-decoupled VLAs found (not in the named list)**
- "Acting While Understanding" — arXiv 2606.15285, 13 Jun 2026 [V-full]: separate understanding and action processes; for pi0.5 the cached object is the vision-language prefix KV cache; semantic refresh free-running at about 3-5 Hz, action replanning every control step (20 Hz loop). Requires retraining of the action module (history conditioning + time-misalignment training). UniVLA LIBERO-Long: sync 92.0, naive cache reuse 85.2, + delay training 89.0, full 91.2. Limitations: "the current fixed semantic refresh schedule is not always optimal"; "Future work includes adaptive semantic refresh and broader validation across VLA architectures." No per-camera treatment. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15285v1)
- Fast-VLA — arXiv 2610.21389 [V-full]: frozen 4B VLM (DM0.5) encodes instruction + initial views once per episode into KV "task memory"; a trained actor with one DINOv2 encoder per camera runs online. Periodic refresh tested only as an ablation (every chunk gives +1.80 on LIBERO, -1.23 on LIBERO-Plus, at 3.12x amortized time). Future work: "Combining Fast-VLA with event-triggered memory updates and high-level replanning could extend it." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21389)
- CloudEdgeVLA — arXiv 2608.00569, 1 Aug 2026 [V-full]: cloud OpenVLA-OFT (7B, LoRA) sends hidden planning features; edge encodes the current frame with frozen SigLIP-Base; trained on paired fresh/delayed frames. At 40-step max delay 63.8-78.0% on LIBERO suites vs at most 6.4% for VLASH. Authors' own counterfactual: current edge vision corrects only about 0.03% of stale-induced action drift. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00569v1)
- "Think at 5 Hz, Act at 20 Hz" — arXiv 2607.15621 [S]: driving; slow VLM exposes a KV cache, fast expert attends to cache + current camera frame, trained under randomized staleness. — [arXiv 2607.15621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15621)
- "Decoupling Vision, Language, and Action" (DEM) — arXiv 2609.18374 [V-full]: no VLM; only the language tokens are cached (language encoder re-run only when the instruction changes; 6.1 ms with cache vs 10.4 ms without); vision is encoded every control step for both cameras. Trained from scratch. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18374)

### Inferences
- The common pattern is "one visual-semantic cache, one clock." Where cameras are mentioned at all (DAM-VLA, D2-VLA, DuoCore-FS, Leap-VLA), they are concatenated before the slow pass. The per-camera element that does exist is orthogonal to refresh timing (D2-VLA's per-camera token quota, Fast-VLA's per-camera encoders, ActPrune's per-view pruning rule).
- All async designs above that report good behaviour under stale caches obtain it by training (delay/offset sampling, age conditioning, stale-frame curricula, adapters). None of them is a drop-in for a frozen pretrained policy.
- Three independent author groups (DAM-VLA, Acting While Understanding, Fast-VLA) and one design-but-not-evaluated path (Leap-VLA) point at change/event-triggered refresh as the next step, which signals the direction is recognised but still unexecuted for the asynchronous cache setting, and not at per-camera granularity anywhere.

### Gaps
- D2-VLA, TIC-VLA, Leap-VLA, SmolVLA, LIBERO-Plus pages were longer than the reader window; appendices of TIC-VLA and SmolVLA were not read. Statements "not mentioned" for those refer to the portion read.
- GR00T N1, VLASH, A2C2, FiS-VLA, HiRT, OpenHelix full texts were not opened; their entries rely on abstracts or secondary summaries.
- GR00T N1.x / Helix publish no per-camera or cache-refresh ablations that could be found.

## 2. Has any paper used different refresh rates for different cameras, camera/view selection at inference, or skipped re-encoding of unchanged views?

### Takeaway
Not found for robot policies at camera granularity. What exists is (a) token-level reuse inside one synchronous forward pass (VLA-Cache, TRIAGE, gated VLA-Cache), (b) whole-observation event triggers that decide when to run the entire VLM/VLA (React When You Need To, StateMem, ElegantVLA, VLA-Corrector), and (c) view-specific token pruning (ActPrune). No paper was found that keeps one camera's features cached while re-encoding another, or that assigns different refresh rates to wrist and third-person cameras.

### Cited Findings

**Token-level, training-free reuse (synchronous)**
- TRIAGE — arXiv 2610.34519, 25 Oct 2026 [V-full] (Kwon, Joe-Wong, Lee; SNU + CMU). Reuses KV states of visual tokens in deep layers (3-31). A token's cache is valid if per-token drift (relative change of layer-2 value vectors since last recompute) is below 0.3 and age is at most T = 10 queries. Importance comes from shallow-layer attention plus a saliency memory. A fixed budget B of tokens is recomputed each step, with a maintenance share (0.06) refreshing the oldest cached tokens. Training-free. OpenVLA-7B and OpenVLA-OFT; LIBERO within 3 points of full recompute (73.7% vs 76.5%; OFT 95.8% vs 97.1%); prefill latency on Jetson AGX Orin about 0.52x on OFT. Per-camera drift is not described; processing is sequential, asynchronous methods are called orthogonal. Limitations: values "are set empirically rather than derived from first principles"; "an adaptive method that adjusts the threshold during deployment is needed"; "TRIAGE detects change only after it happens, through drift." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34519)
- VLA-Cache — see section 1; per-token, per-step, no per-camera statistics. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2)
- "Neural Introspection Gating for Adaptive KV-Cache Reuse in VLA Models" — arXiv 2608.10824 [V-full]. Training-free gate on VLA-Cache: if the mean top-1/top-2 action-token logit margin from the previous step is below a threshold (0.65 OpenVLA, 0.50 OFT), invalidate the cache and run a full pass. OpenVLA LIBERO average 69.9% at 1.53 TFLOPs vs VLA-Cache 68.1% at 1.43 and full 70.1% at 1.87. Single camera image per step; not asynchronous. Limitation: "the logit margin cannot distinguish between cache-induced uncertainty and inherent model ambiguity." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10824)
- FlashVLA ("Think Twice, Act Once") — arXiv 2505.21200 [S]: training-free action reuse gated by action similarity and visual token stability, plus token pruning. — [arXiv 2505.21200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21200v1)
- ActionCache — arXiv 2607.06370 [S]: training-free action-head caching for flow-based VLAs (claims up to 10.44x and 40.17x action-head acceleration). — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2607.06370)

**Whole-observation change/event triggers (decide when to run, not which camera)**
- "React When You Need To: Event-Triggered Asynchronous Inference for VLA Policies" — arXiv 2609.22587, 18 Sep 2026 [V-full] (Wu, Li, Hou, Chen, Knoll). A lightweight Transformer event decoder compares current visual features (from the frozen VLA vision encoder) with anchor features cached at the last policy inference; threshold = 90th percentile of the event-score distribution on static demos; fires only when exceeded "in two consecutive evaluations"; estimator at 15 Hz. Triggers a full policy inference; no KV/prefix cache; three RealSense cameras but no per-camera trigger or re-encoding described. Requires training (pi0.5 LoRA fine-tune + trained event decoder). Two real Franka tasks, 20 trials/setting: 100% / 100% / 85% (average 95%), 55 points above the strongest baseline. Future work: "evaluate the proposed strategy across a broader range of manipulation scenarios and VLA backbones." No mention of AoI, importance weighting or per-camera scheduling. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22587v1)
- StateMem — arXiv 2609.22684, 19 Sep 2026 [V-full]. Caches the VLM prefix KV as a "cache anchor"; a predictor estimates the current perceptual feature from the anchor's memory state and the normalized prediction error is a routing score; reuse when below an online EMA threshold, otherwise refresh the prefix; periodic audits compare cached-prefix vs full-refresh actions. Three RGB views are mean-pooled into one perceptual feature, so the decision is all-or-nothing across cameras. Requires training (two stages, 4 RTX A6000 for about 5 days; pi0.5 init). LIBERO 97.6% with refresh rate 79.75% (only 20.25% of prefix refreshes saved). Limitation: when "refresh rates remain high, routing and audit overhead can offset part of the saved prefix computation." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22684)
- ElegantVLA — arXiv 2605.29438, 28 May 2026 [V-full]. A PPO-trained scheduler (base VLA frozen) picks one of 5 backbone levels (full recompute; refresh vision encoder + first/last LLM layers only; reuse last representation for 1-3 steps) and 3 action-head levels, from CKA similarity of first-LLM-layer hidden states vs a cached anchor, gripper/end-effector speeds and episode progress. GR00T on SimplerEnv 64.00% to 65.88% with up to 2.55x FLOPs speedup; real robot 13.8 Hz to 26.3 Hz. No per-camera scheduling; a fixed threshold rule "transferred poorly across robot suites." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29438v1)
- VLA-Corrector — arXiv 2607.01804 [V-full, first 100k chars]. A latent-space vision monitor compares predicted vs observed latent residuals and truncates the chunk when mismatch stays above a dynamic threshold for p consecutive steps. Backbone frozen but a ~40M residual MLP corrector is trained. pi0.5 MetaWorld 48.70% to 64.35%. Camera not specified; no per-camera treatment. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01804)
- KEMO (arXiv 2606.23589), EventVLA (arXiv 2606.20092), VP-VLA (arXiv 2603.22003) [S]: event/keyframe detection for memory or planner invocation (joint-space slowdowns + DINOv2 difference; learned keyframe probabilities; gripper open/close transitions). — [KEMO](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23589v1), [EventVLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20092), [VP-VLA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22003)
- Leap-VLA trigger path (frame difference / optical flow / gripper events) is designed but not evaluated; DAM-VLA and Fast-VLA name it as future work — see section 1.

**View-specific treatment that is not about refresh timing**
- ActPrune — arXiv 2611.08827, 11 Nov 2026 [V-full]. Training-free, prunes patches before the vision encoder using the previous action chunk: third-person view keeps patches around projected end-effector poses; wrist view reprojects previous patches with depth and prunes unchanged ones into context tokens. Does not cache visual features across steps. OpenVLA-OFT: up to 1.61x speedup and 59.5% FLOPs reduction at 96.1% vs 96.5% LIBERO; pi0.5 only 1.03x speedup because the action expert cost is independent of visual token count. "ActPrune assumes calibrated cameras and depth sensing." No asynchronous inference, per-camera refresh or staleness discussion. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827)
- D2-VLA per-camera token quotas; Fast-VLA per-camera DINOv2 encoders with head camera supplying history — see section 1.
- FlashDrive (driving VLA) — arXiv 2608.12932, 13 Aug 2026 [V-full]. Streaming KV cache with a view-major token layout: encodes only the newest frame per view, keeps preceding frames' KV (keys cached pre-RoPE), streaming attention mask. Requires fine-tuning the action expert; no change-triggered skipping of unchanged views. 716.9 ms to 151.4 ms end-to-end on Alpamayo 1.5-10B. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12932v1)

### Inferences
- TRIAGE is the nearest existing "index" in spirit: per-token validity from drift and age, importance from attention, a per-step recompute budget. It differs from the candidate idea in four ways: token level instead of camera level, synchronous instead of a slow vision thread, no explicit cost term or cross-camera arbitration, and evaluated only on OpenVLA-family autoregressive / parallel-decoding models, not flow-matching prefix-cache models.
- Event-triggered works decide a binary "run the whole model now or not." With N cameras that is a 1-bit decision; the candidate idea is an N-way allocation with a no-op option. No paper was found that makes the N-way decision.
- Several authors report that fixed thresholds transfer poorly (ElegantVLA) or need per-backbone calibration (TRIAGE, gated VLA-Cache), which is a practical risk for any training-free change-score index.

### Gaps
- Queries tried without finding a per-camera refresh paper: "VLA per-camera asynchronous refresh visual token cache wrist camera third-person different rates"; "VLA skip re-encoding unchanged camera view per-view feature cache training-free multi-view view selection inference compute"; "VLA wrist camera higher frequency third-person lower frequency multi-rate visual encoding selective view re-encoding"; "camera-wise OR view-wise OR per-view OR per-camera cache refresh vision-language-action asynchronous cameras different frame rates policy stale view scheduler"; "event-triggered VLM invocation VLA change detection keyframe when to look adaptive perception frequency". Absence in search results is not proof of absence; workshop papers and non-indexed preprints could exist.
- Whether VLA-Cache or TRIAGE in practice reuse more third-person than wrist tokens is not reported.

## 3. Has Age of Information (AoI) / Age of Incorrect Information (AoII) scheduling been applied to robot perception, VLA inference, multi-camera sensing, or edge inference?

### Takeaway
AoI/AoII index policies exist for generic multi-source remote inference and for edge video analytics, but no paper was found that applies AoI/AoII (or a Whittle-type index) to VLA inference, to a learned policy's feature cache, or to multi-camera robot perception. The VLA papers that use "cache age" use it as a conditioning input or validity cap, not as a scheduling objective.

### Cited Findings
- "Timely Communications for Remote Inference" — arXiv 2404.16281 (IEEE/ACM ToN) [V-abs] (Shisher, Sun, Hou). Pre-trained network infers a time-varying target from features sent by a sensing node; "the inference error is a function of Age of Information (AoI), where the function could be non-monotonic"; monotone degradation holds only if the data is close to Markov. Multi-source multi-channel policy = Whittle-index source selection + duality-based "selection-from-buffer," stated asymptotically optimal. Abstract uses video frames as illustrative features. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16281)
- "AoI-based Scheduling of Correlated Sources for Timely Inference" — arXiv 2509.01926 (accepted IEEE ToN special issue) [V-abs] (Shisher, Tripathi, Chiang, Brinton). Correlated sources over a shared channel; restless multi-armed bandit with non-separable penalty because each source's penalty depends on the AoI of other correlated sources; "Maximum Gain First" policy learnt online with bandit feedback when penalty functions are unknown. No cameras or robots mentioned in the abstract. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01926)
- "When and Which Sensor to Observe? Timely Tracking of a Joint Markov Source" — arXiv 2606.30623, 29 Jun 2026 [V-abs] (Cosandal, Ulukus, Akar). Monitor either idles or pulls from one sensor each slot to minimize a weighted sum of average AoII and sampling costs; belief MDP solved by MPC and RL-MPC (not an index policy). Generic sensors; no robotics or camera link. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30623)
- "Age of Incorrect Information for Pull-Based State Estimation of General Markov Sources" — arXiv 2608.13248 [S]: RMAB formulation with an approximate Whittle index policy; theoretical. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13248)
- "A Whittle Index Approach to Minimizing Functions of Age of Information" — arXiv 1908.10438 [S]; "Whittle Index for Scheduling Stochastic Arrivals" — arXiv 1801.03422 [S]: foundational closed-form Whittle indices for AoI cost functions. — [arXiv 1908.10438](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10438), [arXiv 1801.03422](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.03422)
- AoII theory under delay/timeouts: arXiv 2203.02979, 2207.02926, 2209.14254, 2212.14381, 2301.06150 [S]. — [arXiv 2203.02979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02979)
- "Towards Timely Video Analytics Services at the Network Edge" — arXiv 2406.14820 [S]: defines Age of Processed Information (AoPI) blending AoI with recognition accuracy; jointly optimizes edge server selection, per-camera video configuration and resource allocation with a Lyapunov method. Multi-camera, but analytics (detection), not policy inference. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14820)
- "A Novel Indicator for Quantifying and Minimizing Information Utility Loss of Robot Teams" — arXiv 2506.14237 [S]: the only robotics-source AoI-adjacent result surfaced; concerns information exchange in robot teams, not camera sensing for a policy. — [arXiv 2506.14237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14237)
- "Fresh Enough to Decide: Age of Intelligence for Cost-Aware Model Synchronization in Dynamic IoT Systems" — arXiv 2611.09935 [V-abs] (Wang, Hosseinalipour, Chakareski, Shi). Defines Age of Intelligence as decision-relevant distortion of a cached model relative to the current version and trades stale-decision regret against refresh cost; about model weights on IoT devices, not perception caches; no robots/VLA/cameras. Note: the abstract page reported a submission date of 13 Oct 2026 for an ID with a 2611 prefix; this date/ID mismatch was not resolved. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09935)
- VLA-side uses of "age": Leap-VLA conditions the expert on measured cache age (does not use the term AoI; no scheduler) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); D2-VLA feeds normalized elapsed time since refresh to its adapter — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792); TIC-VLA conditions on latency metadata — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459); TRIAGE caps token cache age at T = 10 queries and observes drift grows with age so the cap rarely binds [S for the last clause] — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34519).

### Inferences
- The AoII "pull" formulation of arXiv 2606.30623 (idle or pull one sensor; minimize AoII + sampling cost) is structurally the same decision as "do nothing or re-encode one camera," and arXiv 2509.01926's correlated-source penalty matches the fact that wrist and third-person views are correlated. Neither has been instantiated with a learned policy's cache as the "estimate."
- The non-monotonic-in-age result of arXiv 2404.16281 is a caution: for non-Markov signals, older-but-informative features can beat fresher ones, so a pure age rule is not justified a priori; a change-based (AoII-like) measure is the better-motivated signal.
- A robot-learning paper that names AoI/AoII and derives an index for per-camera refresh would, on this search, be the first to bridge the two literatures.

### Gaps
- Queries tried: "age of information OR age of incorrect information vision-language-action OR robot policy camera scheduling inference"; "age of incorrect information OR age of information multi-camera edge inference scheduling Whittle index robot perception video analytics"; "age of information robot camera sensing scheduling semantic OR task-oriented teleoperation OR embodied age of incorrect information 2025 2026 arXiv"; "information freshness OR age of information vision-language-action OR embodied AI OR VLA inference cache refresh scheduling arXiv 2026"; "age of information edge video analytics multi-camera frame selection inference scheduling". No VLA/robot-policy AoI paper surfaced.
- IEEE Xplore-only papers (not on arXiv) on AoI for teleoperation or networked control of manipulators were not systematically searched.
- Full texts of the AoI papers were not read; whether any uses a robot dataset in experiments is unconfirmed.

## 4. How sensitive are VLA policies to staleness of each input separately (third-person vs wrist vs proprioception)?

### Takeaway
Two September 2026 benchmarks perturb one camera while the other stays current, and both show large drops for off-the-shelf policies; wrist staleness is usually worse than third-person staleness. The conditions tested are harsh (fixed delays of unreported size, freezing from fault onset), so they bound the worst case rather than the short, bounded staleness an adaptive scheduler would produce.

### Cited Findings
- LIBERO-VPro — arXiv 2609.24350, 21 Sep 2026 [V-full] (Li, Mei, Majumdar, Chen, Jiang, Zhu). Camera-staleness perturbations (three fixed-delay levels, observation freezing, historical replay) are "independently applied to either the third-person or wrist camera while the other view remains current." Success (%), normal / third-person stale / wrist stale: OpenVLA-OFT 97.0 / 37.15 / 10.90; pi0 92.5 / 32.85 / 21.15; pi0.5 97.2 / 21.35 / 19.70; FastWAM 98.8 / 21.65 / 12.35; LingBot-VA 97.0 / 55.75 / 13.25; LaWAM 98.5 / 9.95 / 23.25. View removal (third-person removed / wrist removed): OpenVLA-OFT 43.19 / 15.31; pi0 48.75 / 7.62; pi0.5 43.25 / 2.19. "Five of the six models are more sensitive to perturbations of the wrist view." Both-cameras-stale is not reported; step counts of the delay levels were not given in the text read. 40 LIBERO tasks, about 196,000 simulated episodes, 200 real rollouts on Franka Research 3. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24350), [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24350)
- LIBERO-VPro real-world check: delaying the third-person stream by "one replanning cycle" left pi0.5 at 100% on a Move task (LaWAM 80% to 40%), but sharply reduced both models on Pick-and-Place. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24350)
- "Blackout vs. Freeze: Analyzing Physical Failure Modes of VLAs under Camera Faults" — arXiv 2609.39145, 30 Sep 2026 [V-full] (Suh, Han, Gholami, Jo; SNU). Freeze = the frame at fault onset is repeated for the rest of the episode. LIBERO Spatial/Object/Goal average success (%), clean / scene black / wrist black / scene frozen / wrist frozen: pi0.5 with proprio 93.1 / 28.9 / 13.1 / 5.8 / 3.6; pi0.5 without proprio 94.4 / 9.1 / 1.8 / 0.2 / 0.0; GR00T N1.7 with proprio 98.7 / 51.6 / 12.2 / 9.3 / 0.0; GR00T without proprio 97.1 / 34.4 / 0.0 / 5.1 / 0.0 (n = 450 per condition). Freezing is worse than blackout and produces more extreme joint behaviour (GR00T, wrist fault at episode start: joint-velocity-exceedance 3.3% under blackout vs 82.0% under freezing). Blackout training raises scene-freeze success 2.7% to 53.0% and wrist-freeze 0.0% to 30.0%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39145)
- LIBERO-Plus — arXiv 2510.13626 (v3 26 Dec 2025) [V-full, first 100k chars]. With both cameras black, performance "collapses to nearly zero across models"; with only the third-person view black and wrist kept, three models retain 43.6, 43.0 and 67.3 (model names not attached to the numbers in the text read). "the wrist view alone provides critical and stable close-range geometric and contact cues." A wrist-only-masked condition is not tested. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13626)
- Whole-observation delay (not per input): on LIBERO with SmolVLA, naive async drops to ~10-12% at 20-step delay — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168); Leap-VLA's frequency-matched reference drops 97.5% to 61.4% on LIBERO-Spatial when the semantic cache refresh goes from 0.2 s to 2 s — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); UniVLA naive cache reuse 85.2 vs 92.0 sync on LIBERO-Long — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15285v1).
- Proprioception: VLASH's remedy is specifically to roll the proprioceptive state forward while keeping the visual observation fixed — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168); in Blackout-vs-Freeze, removing proprioception makes every camera fault worse (e.g. pi0.5 scene blackout 28.9% with vs 9.1% without) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39145).
- GridS — arXiv 2605.11817 [S]: a wrist-only evaluation (third-person masked/black) reports the baseline "over-relies on the static global view." — [arXiv 2605.11817](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11817)

### Inferences
- Wrist view should generally get the higher refresh priority (5 of 6 models in LIBERO-VPro; wrist removal is near-fatal for pi0/pi0.5), which is consistent with the intuitive "third-person changes slowly, wrist changes fast" prior behind the candidate idea. But third-person staleness is far from free in these benchmarks (pi0.5 drops to 21.35%), so "refresh third-person rarely" cannot be assumed safe without measuring the bounded-staleness regime.
- The existing numbers are not dose-response curves. No paper found reports success as a function of per-camera age in steps or milliseconds for small ages (e.g. 1-10 control steps), which is the regime an index policy operates in. LIBERO-VPro has three delay levels but the per-level numbers and step counts were not obtained.
- LIBERO-VPro's real-robot observation (a one-replanning-cycle third-person delay harmless for a Move task but harmful for Pick-and-Place) suggests staleness tolerance is task- and phase-dependent, supporting an importance-weighted rule rather than fixed per-camera rates.

### Gaps
- Per-delay-level numbers and exact delay sizes in LIBERO-VPro were not retrieved.
- No ablation found that makes proprioception stale while both cameras stay fresh on a VLA, other than as part of whole-observation delay.
- No per-camera staleness data found for SmolVLA or for OpenVLA-OFT outside LIBERO-VPro.

## 5. Do policies trained on synchronized multi-camera frames tolerate temporally inconsistent views (one camera older than the other)?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin and mostly negative for frozen policies: single-camera staleness and freezing cause large success drops, and an open-loop probe shows the policy's action follows whichever camera it currently relies on, which is phase-dependent. One industry blog reports training-time per-camera timestamp jitter (5-30 ms) as a robustness augmentation, with no isolated effect size. No closed-loop study of small, bounded inter-camera age gaps was found.

### Cited Findings
- Blackout vs. Freeze, Appendix E temporal probe [V-full]: each query pairs a scene image and a wrist image taken either now or 10 frames earlier, with instruction and proprioception fixed; the output action is assigned to the nearer of the two time-aligned reference actions. Scene-reference share / wrist-reference share (%): pi0.5 with proprio — early 23.0 / 77.0, near gripper close 41.5 / 58.5, transport 61.5 / 38.5, near episode end 27.7 / 72.3; GR00T with proprio — 11.5 / 88.5, 21.0 / 79.0, 47.5 / 52.5, 30.0 / 70.0. Authors: for pi0.5 "scene-reference following is more frequent during transport (56.5–61.5%)"; "GR00T follows the wrist reference more often in all four groups, with transport closest to an even split." The probe is open-loop (actions queried without execution). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39145)
- LIBERO-VPro: per-camera staleness with the other view current gives the success numbers in section 4; the authors conclude "Strong degradation under both conditions indicates that models rely heavily on the expected temporal and multi-view structure of their inputs," and note "Staleness provides visually valid but outdated observations, whereas view loss removes a source entirely." A separate "cross-view contradiction" dimension (temporally aligned but inconsistent content) drops pi0.5 from 97.2 to 52.5-79.2 depending on contradiction type. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24350)
- Chef Robotics engineering blog, 7 May 2026 [V-abs; practitioner source, not peer-reviewed]: "Three views captured 'simultaneously' show 5-30 ms timestamp differences due to USB scheduling"; as a training augmentation they apply a random temporal offset of 5-30 ms "to each camera's image timestamp independently" and select the nearest older frame. Reported gains (64.9% lower velocity discontinuity, 30.8% lower acceleration jerk) are for the full latency-aware approach, not isolated to camera jitter. Bimanual Trossen robot, 30 Hz, RTX 5090. — [Chef Robotics blog](https://www.chefrobotics.ai/post/latency-aware-vision-language-action-models-vlas-with-system-identification-for-real-time-robot-control)
- Negative training-free evidence on stale prefix KV for a flow-matching VLA: CrossVLA — arXiv 2605.21854 (v2 6 Jun 2026) [V-full]. Reusing pi0.5 prefix past_key_values across timesteps when a "visual signature" is similar: threshold 0.92 gave an 86% hit rate and 0/1 success; threshold 0.98 gave 64% hit rate and 0/2; authors: "stale prefix K/V breaks suffix attention sufficiently that the policy plateaus." Sample sizes are 1-2 episodes per row, so this is anecdotal. Same paper's pi0.5 latency breakdown (single-image observation, H20 GPU): prefix embed + PaliGemma forward about 60 ms (21.4%), 10-step denoise loop about 220 ms (78.6%), total about 280 ms. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21854)
- Positive evidence that robustness to stale/frozen views can be trained in: blackout training lifts wrist-freeze success 0.0% to 30.0% and scene-freeze 2.7% to 53.0% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39145); Leap-VLA's stale-frame masking curriculum recovers 61.4% to 95.5% at 2 s refresh — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); CloudEdgeVLA's paired fresh/stale loss gives 95.1% vs 54.8% with the fresh-only loss — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00569v1).
- Positive training-free evidence at token level: VLA-Cache and TRIAGE mix fresh and previous-step KV states for different tokens within one forward pass and stay within about 0-3 points of full recomputation on OpenVLA / OpenVLA-OFT. — [VLA-Cache](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2), [TRIAGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34519)

### Inferences
- The phase-dependent reference-following in the Appendix E probe is, in effect, a measured per-camera "importance" signal: for pi0.5, the scene camera dominates during transport and the wrist camera dominates elsewhere. An importance term in a refresh index could be estimated the same way (or from gripper state / phase), which no paper has turned into a scheduler.
- There is a tension in the evidence. Token-level reuse of slightly stale KV states works training-free on OpenVLA-family models (reuse is selective and drift-gated), while whole-prefix reuse on pi0.5 reportedly fails (tiny sample) and whole-camera freezing is catastrophic. The candidate idea sits between these: camera-level granularity with change-gated, bounded staleness. Its training-free viability on flow-matching prefix-cache models is therefore an open empirical question, not something existing results settle either way.
- In pi0-style models the image encoder processes each camera independently, so caching per-camera encoder outputs is exact, whereas the prefix KV entries of one camera depend on the other cameras' tokens through prefix self-attention; per-camera reuse at the KV level is an approximation of the kind VLA-Cache/TRIAGE make. This is an architectural inference by the note author, not a claim from any cited paper.

### Gaps
- No closed-loop experiment found with a small, controlled inter-camera age gap (e.g. wrist fresh, third-person k steps old for k = 1..10) on any VLA.
- No peer-reviewed study isolating the effect of per-camera timestamp-jitter augmentation.
- CrossVLA's negative result is not statistically meaningful (1-2 episodes) and its similarity signal is unspecified.

## 6. Which of these works are training-free and which need retraining / fine-tuning?

### Takeaway
Every asynchronous cache-decoupled VLA found needs training; the training-free methods are all synchronous token/action reuse or pruning methods (plus system-level async stacks that do not cache features). A training-free asynchronous vision-cache scheduler was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Training-free, synchronous: VLA-Cache (token KV reuse; OpenVLA, CogACT, OpenVLA-OFT) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02175v2); TRIAGE (drift+age+importance token reuse; OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34519); Neural Introspection Gating (logit-margin gate on VLA-Cache) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10824); ActPrune (per-view pruning; OpenVLA-OFT, pi0.5; needs calibrated cameras + depth) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08827); FlashVLA and ActionCache [S] — [arXiv 2505.21200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21200v1), [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2607.06370).
- Training-free, asynchronous execution but no feature cache: SmolVLA async stack (joint-space similarity filter; model-agnostic) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844); IT-RTC (inference-time inpainting of committed action prefix; roughly triples denoising-loop FLOPs) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168).
- Frozen base + small trained add-on: A2C2 (residual correction head) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168); VLA-Corrector (~40M residual MLP) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01804); ElegantVLA (PPO-trained scheduler, frozen VLA) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29438v1); React When You Need To (trained event decoder; base pi0.5 also LoRA fine-tuned on static demos) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22587v1).
- Retraining / fine-tuning of the policy: DAM-VLA — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105); Leap-VLA — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011); D2-VLA — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34792); TIC-VLA — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02459); DuoCore-FS — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20188); AsyncVLA (navigation) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13476); Acting While Understanding — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15285v1); StateMem — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22684); Fast-VLA (frozen VLM, trained actor) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21389); CloudEdgeVLA — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00569v1); FlashDrive (action expert fine-tuned for streaming) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12932v1); VLASH and TT-RTC — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08168); Helix (end-to-end with training-time temporal offset) — [Figure Helix page](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix); FiS-VLA (dual-aware co-training) — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01953).
- Hardware reported by the training-free cache papers: VLA-Cache RTX 4090; TRIAGE Jetson AGX Orin and RTX 3090; gated VLA-Cache A100; ActPrune RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell. — sources as above.

### Inferences
- "Asynchronous + cached prefix + training-free" is an empty cell in the surveyed literature. The asynchronous cache papers all report that naive stale-cache reuse hurts (Leap-VLA 97.5 to 61.4 at 2 s; UniVLA 92.0 to 85.2) and fix it by training; a training-free method has to keep staleness small enough that this degradation does not appear, which is exactly what a change-triggered per-camera rule aims at.
- Training-free cache methods have so far been validated on OpenVLA-family models; on pi0.5 the reported gains are small (ActPrune 1.03x) because the denoising loop dominates latency (about 79% in CrossVLA's breakdown). In an asynchronous design the vision/prefix pass is off the action-latency path, so the benefit of per-camera refresh there would be lower cache age per unit of vision compute rather than lower action latency — an inference by the note author.

### Gaps
- No training-free asynchronous cache method on pi0 / pi0.5 / SmolVLA was found to compare against.
- Energy or on-device (Jetson-class) measurements exist only for TRIAGE, TIC-VLA, AsyncVLA-navigation; none for a multi-camera async manipulation VLA.

## 7. Verdict for the candidate idea: already done / partially done / open

### Takeaway
**Verdict: partially done in pieces, open as a whole.** Change-triggered refresh, age-aware caches, importance-weighted token reuse and per-camera sensitivity measurements each exist separately, but no paper found (as of 2026-11-16) refreshes an asynchronous VLA's vision cache per camera, arbitrates between cameras with a change x importance / cost index, links that index to AoI/AoII scheduling theory, or does so training-free on a prefix-cache (flow-matching) VLA.

### Cited Findings
Closest five papers and what each leaves open:
- DAM-VLA (arXiv 2606.12105): per-modality asynchronous buffers, but vision is a single buffer on a fixed timer and the policy is retrained; the authors themselves state "A detector that triggers the VLM on change would close this gap." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12105)
- Leap-VLA (arXiv 2611.01011): cache-age conditioning and a designed event-trigger path (frame difference / optical flow / gripper), but "the trigger path is supported by the design but not exercised in any reported cell"; trained; no per-camera slow-cache refresh. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01011)
- TRIAGE (arXiv 2610.34519): training-free reuse governed by per-token drift, age cap and attention importance with a per-step budget — the nearest thing to an index rule — but token-level, synchronous, no cross-camera arbitration or cost term, OpenVLA-family only; authors call for "an adaptive method that adjusts the threshold during deployment." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34519)
- StateMem (arXiv 2609.22684) and React When You Need To (arXiv 2609.22587): change-triggered refresh of the whole prefix KV / whole inference from a feature-change score, but all cameras pooled into one decision and both need training. — [StateMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22684), [React When You Need To](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22587v1)
- LIBERO-VPro (arXiv 2609.24350) and Blackout vs. Freeze (arXiv 2609.39145): the only per-camera staleness evidence; they are benchmarks, not methods, and show frozen policies degrade sharply under harsh single-camera staleness, with wrist usually more critical and reliance shifting by task phase. — [LIBERO-VPro](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24350), [Blackout vs. Freeze](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39145)
- AoI side: Whittle-index and AoII pull-scheduling results exist for generic correlated sources and sensors (arXiv 2404.16281, 2509.01926, 2606.30623) but none targets a learned policy's cache or robot cameras. — [2404.16281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16281), [2509.01926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01926), [2606.30623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30623)

### Inferences
Precise remaining gap (each item was not found in any paper surveyed):
1. Camera-granular refresh in an asynchronous pipeline: keeping camera A's cached features/KV while re-encoding camera B, with different effective rates per camera.
2. An N-way arbitration rule with a no-op option (which camera, or none) using change-since-last-encode, an importance weight and an explicit compute cost; existing triggers are binary and whole-observation.
3. An explicit AoI/AoII formulation (restless bandit / index policy) for a policy's perception cache, bridging the networking literature and VLA inference.
4. Training-free operation on a frozen prefix-cache VLA (pi0 / pi0.5 / SmolVLA class) in the asynchronous setting; all async cache designs retrain, and the training-free cache methods are synchronous and OpenVLA-centric.
5. Dose-response data: success vs per-camera cache age in the small-age regime, per task phase.

Main risks the existing evidence raises for the idea:
- Frozen policies are fragile to single-camera staleness in harsh settings (LIBERO-VPro: pi0.5 97.2 to about 20 with either camera stale; Blackout vs. Freeze: near zero under freezing), and the one whole-prefix-reuse attempt on pi0.5 failed (CrossVLA, 1-2 episodes). The idea is only viable if change-gating keeps effective staleness well inside what these tests probe; that must be shown, not assumed.
- Thresholds/indices calibrated on one backbone or suite transfer poorly (ElegantVLA's fixed-rule baseline; TRIAGE and gated VLA-Cache per-model calibration).
- For flow-matching VLAs the denoising loop dominates latency, so the payoff must be framed as fresher caches per unit vision compute (or energy on edge hardware), not faster action generation.
- Novelty window: three groups flagged change-triggered refresh as future work between June and November 2026, so a whole-observation version is likely to appear soon; the per-camera arbitration and AoII-index framing are the parts with the least prior art.
