The observation under test: "lerobot 0.5.1 + hf-libero 0.1.4 + robosuite 1.4.0; libero_spatial task 5 ('pick up the black bowl on the ramekin and place it on the plate') fails 0/120 under MuJoCo 3.8.1 but succeeds under MuJoCo 3.2.3 with lerobot/smolvla_libero; cause = the ~10 no-op settle steps after loading an above-support init state leave the bowl ~3.7 cm off-centre and tilted under 3.8.1; suite-level 78% -> 86%."

## 1. Are there issues / posts / papers reporting that LIBERO success rates depend on MuJoCo, robosuite or LIBERO package versions, or that a specific task (black bowl on the ramekin) fails systematically?

### Takeaway
Yes. The exact phenomenon is already publicly documented, most completely in huggingface/lerobot issue #4390 (opened 2026-08-09): MuJoCo >= 3.4.0 makes the libero_spatial task-5 bowl settle tilted on the ramekin rim with 3.72 cm XY drift (vs 1.45 cm, seated, for <= 3.3.7), with per-policy success drops and a same-physics A/B that isolates the initial state as the cause. Earlier and parallel reports exist (LIBERO #141 from 2026-04-16, SimVLA #14, LIBERO #88, an independent 40-task drift atlas, one arXiv paper), but there is no peer-reviewed paper dedicated to it, and the fix (PR #4465) is still unmerged.

- Title: "mujoco>=3.4.0 silently breaks the initial-state premise of libero_spatial task 5 (bowl no longer seats on the ramekin during reset), and the current pin <3.9.0 does not guard against it". Author Rick0525, opened 2026-08-09, state OPEN, labels include bug/dependencies/evaluation/simulation, maintainer Maximellerbach self-assigned 2026-11-13. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Environment in the report: LeRobot 0.6.0, Python 3.12.13, robosuite 1.4.1, MuJoCo swapped per run across 3.2.7, 3.3.0, 3.3.7, 3.4.0, 3.6.0, 3.8.1; second evidence arm RLinf v0.3 with official openvla-oft checkpoints. - Mechanism as stated: the stored task-5 init states place the bowl about 11 cm above the ramekin and rely on the physics settle inside `env.reset()`; under MuJoCo >= 3.4.0 the bowl settles tilted on the ramekin rim with about half of it overhanging instead of seating. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- No-policy probe (all 50 init states, identical within each version): MuJoCo 3.2.7 / 3.3.0 / 3.3.7 -> seated, XY drift 1.45 cm; MuJoCo 3.4.0 / 3.6.0 / 3.8.1 -> tilted on rim, XY drift 3.72 cm. Boundary is exactly 3.4.0 (released 2025-12-05). Example probe output: 3.8.1 `xy_cm_mean` 3.72, `tilt_deg_mean` 10.5; 3.3.0 `xy_cm_mean` 1.45, `tilt_deg_mean` 16.0. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Proposed root cause: the only contact-related entry in the 3.4.0 changelog is a box-box distance bugfix (commit `88383684`, `engine_collision_box.c`); the bowl is 40 box geoms and the ramekin 25 box geoms, so seating is pure box-box contact; the reporter argues the "healthy" seating depended on the pre-3.4.0 bug, and says the 3.9.0 margin/gap redesign was ruled out. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Policy impact on task 5 over all 50 init states (healthy vs broken physics): SmolVLA 0.45B finetune (LeRobot) 80% @3.2.7 -> 28% @3.8.1; pi0.5 (`pi05_libero_finetuned`) 90% -> 86%; OpenVLA-OFT + GRPO (RLinf, greedy, 500 eps) 98% @3.2.7 and @3.3.0 -> 12% @3.8.1 and 19% @3.9.0; OpenVLA-OFT full SFT (official ckpt) 96% @3.2.7 -> 52% @3.9.0. Whole-suite scores shift "by up to about 9 pp" from this single task. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Same-physics A/B: with MuJoCo held at 3.8.1 and only the task-5 init file replaced by a re-settled one, SmolVLA 28% -> 84% and OFT-GRPO 6/50 -> 49/50, i.e. the version effect is carried by the initial state, not by in-episode physics. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Proposed fixes: (1) short term, tighten pin to `mujoco<3.4.0`; (2) root fix, regenerate pre-settled init states (settle once with robot DOFs pinned, keep arm pose, zero velocities, time = 0), byte-compatible with `.pruned_init`. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Independent replication in the thread (clavis-systems, 2026-10-21; LeRobot 0.6.1; MuJoCo 3.3.7, 3.4.0, 3.8.1, 3.12.0; CPU only, no policy): task 5 matches (1.45 cm @3.3.7; 3.72 cm @3.4.0 and 3.8.1); 3.4.0 and 3.8.1 agree to 5e-12 m; in MuJoCo 3.12.0 (box-box collider rewritten) task 5 changes again to 6.23 cm. — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)

### Inferences
- The user's observation is a re-discovery of #4390. The 3.7 cm figure matches the published 3.72 cm; the "tilted" description matches; the working version 3.2.3 lies below the published 3.4.0 boundary; the broken version 3.8.1 is one of the published broken versions.
- A suite-level change 78% -> 86% (8 pp) is what one task out of ten flipping from ~0% to ~80% produces, consistent with #4390's "up to about 9 pp".
- arXiv 2609.37771 attributes the pi0.5 "pick up bowl" 62% -> 88% change between MuJoCo 3.8.1 and 3.3.2 to *rendering*. Since 3.3.2 and 3.8.1 straddle both the 3.3.3 rendering change and the 3.4.0 settle change, that attribution is confounded; #4390's A/B (same MuJoCo, re-settled init file restores the score) suggests the settle effect is at least part of it. This is an inference; the paper does not name the task.
- Two distinct MuJoCo-version channels are documented: visual (>= 3.3.3: sRGB texture / lighting changes, LIBERO #88) and physical (>= 3.4.0: box-box distance fix changes how dropped objects settle). A third is plain API breakage (MuJoCo 3.16 + robosuite 1.4.0 fails at reset).

## 2. Which MuJoCo and robosuite versions do the main LIBERO users pin? Which MuJoCo changelog entries between 3.2.x and 3.8.x plausibly affect how stacked objects settle?

### Takeaway
Only openpi's LIBERO example pins MuJoCo exactly (3.2.3); original LIBERO, OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT pin robosuite but leave MuJoCo floating, and hf-libero 0.1.4 allows `mujoco>=3.0.0,<3.9.0`, which resolves to 3.8.1. The MuJoCo changelog has exactly one contact entry at the reported 3.4.0 boundary ("Fixed a bug in the box-box distance computation"), plus several other default changes in 3.3.0 - 3.9.0 that could alter results.

- 3.9.0 (May 27, 2026): contact `margin`/`gap` semantics redesigned (detection when `dist < margin + gap`, force when `dist < margin`); "With the default values margin = 0, gap = 0, the behavior is unchanged." — [MuJoCo changelog](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)
- 3.12.0 (August 20, 2026): "Rewrote cleaner box-box SAT collider."; fixed box-box bug where near-degenerate face clipping produced spuriously large penetration depth "causing resting stacks to explode"; fixed box-box bug where deep penetrations "could produce no contacts, letting boxes tunnel through thin boxes. Fixes issue 1800." — [MuJoCo changelog](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)
- 4.0.0 (November 6, 2026) [PAGE]: nativeccd/multiccd stability improvements that "can slightly change contacts for models with convex or mesh collisions"; contact pairs with undefined solref now mix via solmix; RK4 changes for free joints. 4.0.1 (November 11, 2026) fixes a sparse Cholesky regression in the Newton solver. — [MuJoCo changelog](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)

### Inferences
- The user's "works" version 3.2.3 is exactly the version openpi locks for its LIBERO example, i.e. the version the pi0/pi0.5 LIBERO numbers were produced with; the "fails" version 3.8.1 is what `pip install` resolves for hf-libero 0.1.4 (highest release below 3.9.0). So the regression is the default outcome of a fresh lerobot install after 2026-05-11, and of any unpinned install after 2025-12-05 (3.4.0).
- Between 3.2.3 and 3.8.1 there are at least four result-changing default switches (nativeccd default in 3.3.0, box-box fix in 3.4.0, margin/gap summing in 3.5.0, multiccd default in 3.8.0). The drift-atlas finding that 3.4.0 and 3.8.1 agree to 5e-12 m for the start states argues that only the 3.4.0 change matters for the 10-step settle, since LIBERO objects are box-decomposed (box-box collider, not the convex CCD pipeline). That is an inference from two sources, not a tested statement about in-episode dynamics.
- The pins in the hf-libero 0.1.4 metadata (`<3.9.0`) guard against the 3.9.0 margin/gap change but not against the 3.4.0 change, which is the point of #4390's title.

### Gaps
- robosuite 1.4.0 / 1.4.1's own `mujoco` requirement was not read from metadata. hf-libero 0.1.3's MuJoCo requirement (the version lerobot 0.5.1's lower bound allows) was not checked.
- Which MuJoCo version the original LIBERO demonstrations and `.pruned_init` files were generated with (2023) is not documented anywhere I found; nor is the MuJoCo version used for the OpenVLA "modified_libero_rlds" regeneration.
- No MuJoCo maintainers' statement was found linking commit `88383684` to stacked-object behaviour; mujoco#3476 (box-box rewrite) is cited by the drift atlas but was not opened.
- lerobot `main` `src/lerobot/envs/libero.py` returned 404 at the path valid for v0.5.1 (file moved); only the v0.5.1 file was read.

## 3. What do LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-X and similar robustness benchmarks show about brittleness to small initial-position perturbations, and do they discuss simulator-version effects?

### Takeaway
All three show that policies scoring 90%+ on stock LIBERO collapse under modest object-position or robot-initial-state changes (LIBERO-PRO: OpenVLA and pi0 to 0.0% under position perturbation; LIBERO-Plus: OpenVLA 76.5% -> 4.1% under robot-init and 31.6% under layout; LIBERO-X: best model 65.2% at the "minor position variation" level). None of them mentions MuJoCo/robosuite versions, physics settling or init-state files; they explain why a 3.7 cm unintended displacement is enough to zero a task, but they do not document the version effect.

### Cited Findings
- LIBERO-PRO (arXiv 2510.03827; Xueyang Zhou et al.; v2 stamped 25 May 2026): perturbation types O (object attributes), R (initial configuration / object positions), L (instruction paraphrase), T (task), E (environment). Abstract: models with "over 90% accuracy" on standard LIBERO see "their performance collapses to 0.0%" in the generalized setting, attributed to "rote memorization of action sequences and environment layouts from the training set". Section 2.3: for OpenVLA and pi0 "task success collapses once the displacement exceeds 0.2 units"; pi0.5 holds to roughly 0.4 units (units not stated). Section 5.2: pi0.5 reaches 0.38 on libero-goal under position changes versus 0 for OpenVLA and pi0. 50 episodes per task. No mention of MuJoCo version, robosuite version, settling, `num_steps_wait` or init files. [PAGE] — [arXiv 2510.03827v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03827v2)

### Inferences
- The robustness literature gives the mechanism for the size of the effect: a policy that has memorised a scene layout fails when the target object is displaced by a few centimetres or tilted. The MuJoCo >= 3.4.0 settle is, in effect, an unintended LIBERO-PRO-style position perturbation applied to one task (and to libero_10 tasks 0/1/7) by the dependency resolver.
- pi0.5's small drop in #4390 (90% -> 86%) versus SmolVLA (80% -> 28%) and OpenVLA-OFT (98% -> 12-19%) has the same ordering as LIBERO-PRO / LIBERO-X position robustness (pi0.5 most robust). This is a consistency observation across sources, not something any source states.
- None of these benchmarks pins or reports the MuJoCo version, so their own absolute numbers may also carry a version dependence (the audit paper found that for stock LIBERO).

### Gaps
- LIBERO-PRO per-suite, per-perturbation numbers are in an image (Figure 7) and were not extracted; the physical size of "0.2 units" is not stated in the text read.
- LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-X do not give the perturbation magnitude in centimetres in the text read, so a direct "3.7 cm vs benchmark perturbation size" comparison could not be made.
- The last ~10-15% of the LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-X HTML pages (appendices) was not read.

## 4. How are LIBERO initial states generated and used, and is the pre-settle (evaluation) vs post-settle (training data) mismatch discussed?

### Takeaway
Every mainstream evaluator loads a stored MuJoCo state per episode with `env.set_init_state(...)` and then steps 10 no-op actions "to wait for objects to stabilize" before the policy sees anything; the stored state for the ramekin task has the bowl in mid-air, so the scene the policy starts from is computed by whatever MuJoCo is installed. The mismatch is discussed in LIBERO #141, lerobot #4390, LIBERO #131 and (for LIBERO-PRO) arXiv 2609.37771, and pre-settled init files have been proposed, but no upstream fix has landed.

### Cited Findings
- OpenVLA `run_libero_eval.py` [RAW]: `num_steps_wait: int = 10  # Number of steps to wait for objects to stabilize in sim`; `num_trials_per_task: int = 50`; `seed: int = 7`; `initial_states = task_suite.get_task_init_states(task_id)`; `obs = env.set_init_state(initial_states[episode_idx])`; during `t < cfg.num_steps_wait` it steps `get_libero_dummy_action(...)`. — [openvla run_libero_eval.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openvla/openvla/main/experiments/robot/libero/run_libero_eval.py)
- openpi `examples/libero/main.py` [RAW]: same structure; `num_steps_wait: int = 10`, `num_trials_per_task: int = 50`, `seed: int = 7`, `replan_steps: int = 5`, `obs = env.set_init_state(initial_states[episode_idx])`; and the comment `env.seed(seed)  # IMPORTANT: seed seems to affect object positions even when using fixed initial state`. — [openpi main.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/main/examples/libero/main.py)
- lerobot v0.5.1 `src/lerobot/envs/libero.py` [RAW]: init states are read with `torch.load(init_states_path, weights_only=False)` from `get_libero_path("init_states") / ... / task_suite.tasks[i].init_states_file`; `num_steps_wait: int = 10`; `reset()` calls `self._env.seed(seed)`, then `self._env.set_init_state(self._init_states[self.init_state_id % len(self._init_states)])`, then `self.init_state_id += self._reset_stride`, then `for _ in range(self.num_steps_wait): self._env.step(get_libero_dummy_action())`. — [lerobot v0.5.1 libero.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/lerobot/v0.5.1/src/lerobot/envs/libero.py)
- Training-data side, OpenVLA `regenerate_libero_dataset.py` [RAW]: the regenerated ("no_noops") datasets are produced by replaying demos: `env.reset(); env.set_init_state(orig_states[0]); for _ in range(10): env.step(get_libero_dummy_action("llava"))` ("Reset environment, set initial state, and wait a few steps for environment to settle"), then re-executing the recorded actions; images saved at 256x256 instead of 128x128; no-op actions and unsuccessful demos filtered out; `orig_states[0]` (the demo's first stored state, not the benchmark `.pruned_init` file) is saved as `initial_state`. — [openvla regenerate_libero_dataset.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openvla/openvla/main/experiments/robot/libero/regenerate_libero_dataset.py)
- The LeRobot docs list the training datasets `lerobot/libero` and `HuggingFaceVLA/libero` (1,693 episodes / 273,465 frames / 40 tasks, 2 x 256x256 cameras) and name `physical-intelligence/libero` as "the original dataset published by Physical Intelligence". [PAGE] — [LeRobot LIBERO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/libero)
- Stored eval state for the ramekin task: bowl at z = 1.08 vs ramekin at z = 0.97 in `pruned_init` (LIBERO #141); "about 11 cm above the ramekin", relying on the settle in `reset()` (#4390). [PAGE] — [LIBERO #141](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/issues/141); [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)
- Settling is large everywhere, not only on task 5: "In 39 of 40 tasks, every state has an object that moves about 4 to 16 cm during settling"; in libero_10 tasks 0/1/7 some objects (ketchup) are saved inside the table and are ejected (to 1.14 m under >= 3.4.0) before landing. [PAGE] — [libero-mujoco-drift-atlas](https://github.com/clavis-systems/libero-mujoco-drift-atlas)
- Why it is invisible: the settle happens before any frame is rendered, so evaluation videos already start with the displaced bowl. [PAGE] — [LIBERO #141](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/issues/141)
- Proposed remedies in public: pre-settled init files (settle once with robot DOFs pinned, keep arm pose, zero velocities, time = 0), reported to restore scores under MuJoCo 3.8.1 (SmolVLA 28% -> 84%; OFT-GRPO 6/50 -> 49/50) — [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390); lowering gravity to -3 during initialization (user workaround) — [LIBERO #131](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/issues/131); pinning `mujoco<3.4.0` — [lerobot PR #4465](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4465)
- Init-state bookkeeping bugs in lerobot (independent of physics): #4152 (Ono-Katsuki, 2026-07-26, lerobot 0.6.1, OPEN): `LiberoEnv.step()` resets internally on termination and gymnasium `NEXT_STEP` autoreset resets again, each advancing `init_state_id`, so which init states a policy sees depends on when it terminates; the seed does not select the init state because `set_init_state` overwrites the simulator state; `truncated` is always False. A reconstructed paired comparison went from McNemar p = 0.003 to p = 0.092 on the genuinely paired episodes. [PAGE] — [lerobot #4152](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4152)
- Earlier lerobot init-state bugs referenced by others: #2375 (rollouts always starting from the same initial state, fixed by PR #2899) and #2850 (`eval.batch_size` capped the number of unique init states) [PAGE, via #4152 and the HF blog] — [lerobot #4152](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4152); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/zuoxingdong/smolvla-libero-spatial-eval); before a 19 January 2026 LeRobot fix (PR #2817) `set_init_state()` ran before `env.reset()`, so evaluations used freshly sampled scenes [PAGE] — [libero-vla-eval](https://github.com/lhtrongai/libero-vla-eval)
- `set_init_state()` does not fully determine the scene: fixture placement (wooden cabinet, stove, wine rack) is not restored and can differ by up to about 2 cm between seeds [PAGE] — [libero-vla-eval](https://github.com/lhtrongai/libero-vla-eval); robosuite's placement sampler draws from the global RNG, leaving about 1.8 mean pixel variation between "identical" rollouts [PAGE] — [faultline](https://github.com/amanshah0729/faultline)
- LeRobot docs on reset modes: soft resets (`--env.init_states=true --env.hard_reset=false`) "are not bit-identical to hard resets after the environment's settling steps, so camera observations and policy results may differ slightly. Use hard resets when reproducing benchmark results." [PAGE] — [LeRobot LIBERO docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/libero)

### Inferences
- The training data and the evaluation start state come from different stored states: training demos replay `demo["states"][0]` (then 10 settle steps under the dataset creator's MuJoCo), while evaluation loads `.pruned_init` (then 10 settle steps under the evaluator's MuJoCo). Both depend on the settle, so the "dataset look" of the bowl-on-ramekin scene is itself a product of an old MuJoCo. No source I found measures the settled eval scene against the dataset frames directly; #4390 compares MuJoCo versions with each other and the drift atlas explicitly says its 3.3.7 reference "is a reference, not ground truth".
- Whether 10 steps is enough to settle is a second, separate question: #4390's probe settled for 250 steps with the robot pinned, the drift atlas used the 10 steps lerobot uses and notes the ketchup only reaches rest height "by step 20". A policy may therefore start acting while objects are still moving in some libero_10 tasks.
- The LIBERO #131 gravity workaround indicates users had hit settle-related bad starts by January 2026 without connecting it to the MuJoCo version.

### Gaps
- How the original LIBERO authors generated `.init` / `.pruned_init` (sampling procedure, the pruning criterion, MuJoCo version) was not found in a primary source; the LIBERO paper and repo docs were not read for this.
- Whether the first stored demo state for the ramekin task also has the bowl in mid-air (i.e. whether training data went through the same drop) was not verified; LIBERO #71 ("states" in hdf5 demo files) may cover it but was not read.
- No source quantifies the pixel- or pose-level gap between the dataset's first frames and the evaluation start scene under each MuJoCo version.

### Inferences
- For the public `lerobot/smolvla_libero` checkpoint under a default fresh install, two independent defects stack on libero_spatial: the shipped `n_action_steps=50` (about -17 to -28 pp) and MuJoCo 3.8.1 (task 5 at 0%, about -8 pp). A suite-level 78% -> 86% is in line with the 76.7% (with task 5 broken) and 85.2% (excluding task 5) figures in the #4614 replication at `n_action_steps=10`.
- lerobot's 280-step limit for libero_spatial vs 220 in OpenVLA/openpi means lerobot Spatial numbers are measured under a more generous time budget than OpenVLA/openpi numbers. I found no source that quantifies the effect.
- With 10 episodes per task (the SmolVLA paper protocol), a single task going 8/10 -> 0/10 is visible, but most of the other pitfalls (few-pp shifts) are inside the Wilson interval, which is why they survived unnoticed.

## 6. Papers on simulator-version or physics-engine sensitivity of robot-learning benchmarks in general

### Takeaway
There is documentation-level and benchmark-paper-level acknowledgement that simulator and benchmark versions change results (Gymnasium MuJoCo docs, Meta-World+), and one 2026 audit paper lists unpinned MuJoCo as a LIBERO reproducibility bug, but I found no paper that systematically studies physics-engine version sensitivity of manipulation benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Gymnasium MuJoCo docs: the exact behaviour of the MuJoCo simulator changes slightly between mujoco versions due to floating-point operation ordering; training performance of v2/v3 and v4 environments is not directly comparable because of the change to the newer simulator; v5 is recommended, v4 maintained for reproducibility. [SEARCH] — [Gymnasium MuJoCo docs](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco)
- A third-party benchmark (SLM-Lab) states Gymnasium MuJoCo v5 environments are significantly harder than v4 and expects lower scores on v5 (its own 10-30% claim). [SEARCH] — [SLM-Lab continuous benchmark](https://slm-lab.gitbook.io/slm-lab/benchmark-results/continuous-benchmark)
- Meta-World+ (arXiv 2505.11289; Reginald McLean et al.; v1 2025-05-16, v2 2025-11-21; NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks): "numerous undocumented changes which inhibit a fair comparison of algorithms"; "inconsistencies with the versioning of the benchmark"; the release exposes both V1 and V2 reward functions and recommends re-running baselines on the benchmark version in use instead of quoting older numbers. [SEARCH] — [arXiv 2505.11289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11289)
- arXiv 2609.37771 (2026-09-29): classifies benchmark bugs into task consistency, initialization and reproducibility; states "LIBERO leaves MuJoCo unpinned, allowing version changes to alter rendered observations"; bug fixes "can reverse method rankings". [PAGE] — [arXiv 2609.37771](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37771)
- MuJoCo's own changelog marks result-changing releases explicitly, e.g. 4.0.0: nativeccd/multiccd improvements "can slightly change contacts for models with convex or mesh collisions"; 3.3.0: nativeccd default "may break some workflows". [RAW/PAGE] — [MuJoCo changelog](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changelog.html)
- The drift atlas mentions a tool/project called "simblame" as related work (not opened, nature unknown). [PAGE] — [libero-mujoco-drift-atlas](https://github.com/clavis-systems/libero-mujoco-drift-atlas)

### Inferences
- The LIBERO case is unusual in this literature because the version effect is not floating-point noise or a reward-definition change but a discrete change in a collider that flips a scene's qualitative state, amplified by policies that memorise layouts. The closest general statements (Gymnasium, Meta-World+) are about RL benchmarks, not imitation-learned VLAs evaluated from stored states.

### Gaps
- Only one search was run for general simulator-version papers (query: "MuJoCo version change affects benchmark results reproducibility robot learning Gymnasium mujoco v4 v5 Meta-World version inconsistencies paper") plus one for Meta-World+; Gymnasium and Meta-World+ were not opened (search-summary level). Papers on cross-simulator transfer (sim-to-sim), robosuite v1.4 -> v1.5 result changes, or ManiSkill/Isaac version effects were not searched and may exist.

## Verdict: is the MuJoCo-version effect on LIBERO already publicly documented?

### Takeaway
**Yes — documented, in substance and in detail, but only in grey literature (GitHub issues, READMEs, one blog) and not yet fixed upstream.** The specific task, the 3.72 cm tilted-on-rim settle, the settle-during-reset mechanism, the version boundary (3.4.0), a candidate MuJoCo commit, multi-policy success drops, and a same-physics A/B were published on 2026-08-09 in lerobot #4390; the symptom was first reported on 2026-04-16 in LIBERO #141. Peer-reviewed / arXiv coverage is partial: arXiv 2609.37771 lists unpinned MuJoCo as a LIBERO bug but attributes it to rendering.

### Cited Findings
- Closest source, matches the observation point by point: [lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390) (2026-08-09, open; fix [PR #4465](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/4465) open as of 2026-11-16).
- First symptom report: [LIBERO #141](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/issues/141) (2026-04-16, MuJoCo 3.6.0, ~1.6 cm slide; "bowls are placed correctly in older MuJoCo versions").
- 0% on task 5 with the same public checkpoint (`lerobot/smolvla_libero`) at MuJoCo 3.8.1: 0/15 in [lerobot #4614](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4614) (comment of 2026-09-21).
- Independent no-policy replication over all 40 tasks with the same stack as the user's (robosuite 1.4.0, hf-libero 0.1.4): [libero-mujoco-drift-atlas](https://github.com/clavis-systems/libero-mujoco-drift-atlas).
- Suite-level version sensitivity without mechanism: [SimVLA #14](https://github.com/LUOyk1999/SimVLA/issues/14) (98.1% @3.1.0 vs ~91% @3.9.0); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/zuoxingdong/smolvla-libero-spatial-eval) (75.5% @3.3.2 vs 72.5% @3.4.0).
- arXiv-level mention: [arXiv 2609.37771](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37771) (pin 3.3.2 instead of 3.8.1; pi0.5 "pick up bowl" 62% -> 88%; attributed to rendered observations).
- Visual channel, earlier: [LIBERO #88](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/issues/88) (2025-06-19, darker floors from MuJoCo 3.3.3).

### Inferences
What would still be new in reporting the user's observation:
- The exact stack **lerobot 0.5.1 + hf-libero 0.1.4 + robosuite 1.4.0** with a policy in the loop (#4390 used lerobot 0.6.0 + robosuite 1.4.1; the drift atlas used robosuite 1.4.0 + hf-libero 0.1.4 but no policy).
- A **MuJoCo 3.2.3** data point, which is the version openpi locks; the lowest version tested publicly for the settle is 3.2.7 (SimVLA #14 mentions 3.1.0 and 2.3.2 for suite averages only).
- A larger sample for the public checkpoint: **0/120** versus the public 0/15, and a suite-level **78% -> 86%** pair for `lerobot/smolvla_libero` (the public suite figures are 76.7% vs 85.2%-excluding-task-5 in one replication, and "about 3.6 pp" / "up to about 9 pp" statements).
- A direct comparison of the settled evaluation scene with the **training dataset** frames/states ("a scene absent from the training data" / "settles as in the dataset"). Public sources compare MuJoCo versions against each other; none measures against the dataset, and the drift atlas says its reference is not ground truth.
- Still open and unreported anywhere I found: policy-level impact on libero_10 tasks 0, 1 and 7 (drift documented, no success rates); a commit-level bisect confirming `88383684`; the effect under MuJoCo 3.9 - 4.0.1 with a policy for the lerobot stack; and whether 10 settle steps suffice.
- It would not be new to claim discovery of the mechanism, the 3.4.0 boundary, the 3.7 cm figure, or the pre-settled-init-file remedy; those should be cited to #4390 / LIBERO #141.

### Gaps
- All GitHub evidence was read through a summarizing fetch, not verbatim; before citing, the numbers in #4390, #4614 and #141 should be re-read on the pages.
- Searches covered GitHub issues (REST search, queries listed in section 1), arXiv via web search, one HF blog and PyPI; I did not search X/Twitter, Discord, Reddit, Zhihu/CSDN or OpenReview, where further informal reports may exist.
- No statement from the LIBERO authors, the LeRobot maintainers (beyond self-assignment on 2026-11-13) or the MuJoCo team on this effect was found.
